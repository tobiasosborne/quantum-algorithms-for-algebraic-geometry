The initial candidate is a retained-label octonionic associator phase.
Basis multiplication has `e_a e_b=(-1)^F(a,b)e_(a+b)` for binary 3-vectors.
Comparing two bracketings while retaining input labels is claimed to give
the deterministic phase `(-1)^(det(a,b,c))`. Check an explicit multiplication
convention, the actual reversible circuit and the relation between basis-label
nonassociativity and continuous octonion or spinor geometry.

Root's concrete attacks to verify:

- A Hadamard-test mean of a diagonal determinant phase is reproduced by
  separately measuring input basis labels and evaluating the phase classically.
  That does not simulate arbitrary coherent later readout.
- The associator is explicitly the group-cochain coboundary `delta F`.
  Do not conflate its determinant formula with a nontrivial type-III
  Dijkgraaf--Witten cocycle. For a compatible flat labelling on a closed
  triangulated 3-manifold, test whether the product telescopes to one;
  boundaries may retain the known F factors.
- A fixed three-bit multiplication algebra does not supply a growing-family
  speedup. Exact reduction to a cubic Boolean phase/IQP circuit is substantive
  only when the whole claimed mechanism and its readout are identified.
- If the author derives a second operation, audit its actual input promise,
  contraction norm, retained failures, classical output and same-input cost.
