# Bounded construction: a quantum theta-isogeny operation

Read CLAUDE.md, PRD criterion 6 and D22--D24, and HANDOFF.md. Root owns
tracking (`qaag-ibb`) and shared files. No new subagents. Write only
`scouting/theta-isogeny-r6.md`. You may finish the already assigned minor

Derive an actual operation from the isogeny
`mu(P,Q)=(P+Q,P-Q)` on a symmetric principally polarized abelian variety.
Start from `mu^*(L^k box L^k)=L^(2k) box L^(2k)` and its finite character
sectors. Do not assume these twisted sectors are canonically identical.
Derive the metric, normalized reproducing-kernel identity and exact action
on two evaluation states. Retain sector labels with their actual weights.

Root's initial attacks to resolve:

- Torsion-character summands generally carry flat twists. Identifying them
  with ordinary level-k sections may need point translations outside the
  level-k theta group; those are not automatically unitary corrections.
- A section-space direct sum is not by itself a cheaply compiled isometry.
  State the basis, polarization and period-matrix access and charge them.
- Do not assume finite-level Bergman densities are exactly constant under
  every translation. Check the actual evaluation normalization.
- Product elliptic curves with a standard theta structure may reduce to
  finite arithmetic, Fourier or Weil operations, or an optical beam splitter
  on an encoded oscillator. Derive the exact reduction if it exists.
- A new point encoding alone fails D22; require a concrete same classical
  output problem and a mechanism beyond described transforms.

Work on one explicit family first (genus one is a useful diagnostic, but
not automatically a hard family). State gates, live memory, copy access,
failure probabilities, accuracy and a matched classical attack. Derive
before targeted primary-source novelty checks. Keep the memo compact;
one resolved construction beats a large menu. If the diagnostic collapses,
state the exact remaining lemma for a genuinely different family without
claiming a universal impossibility theorem.
