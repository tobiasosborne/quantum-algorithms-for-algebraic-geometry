# A quantum copy advantage for selecting generators through their syzygies

2026-09-07. Root construction under the user's revised PRD D25 criterion.
Canonical definitions are in `definitions/syzygy-plucker-r12.md`. This memo
is submitted for independent review in `verdicts/syzygy-advantage-r12.md`.
Independent review has passed; C-374--C-376 are PROVED. It uses established
antisymmetric comparison and the already audited C-362
lower bound. Under D25, using those nonexcluded operations is eligible;
unprecedented mechanism authorship is not asserted.

The strengthened principal task is now D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-OUTPUT: find ANY
pair of original labels generating the full ideal with success at least 2/3.
Section 5A proves an eight-copy quantum upper and an unconditional adaptive
separate-copy lower for this simpler task. The twenty-copy volume-law result
is retained as an additional, more constrained sampling output.

## 1. The computational task and why the based tuple matters

Given the fixed bath-copy source for an unknown redundant tuple of quadratic
generators, choose two generator labels with probability proportional to their
squared Fock area. Every pair of positive target weight generates the same
nonreduced ideal as the full tuple. The unknown constant syzygies tell which
linear combinations of the labelled equations are redundant.

This is a concrete column/basis-selection task with an exact output law. It
does not ask for a new invariant of the bare ideal `(z0²,z1²)`, which is fixed
and known. It asks about the based presentation supplied as quantum data.
Volume weighting is a standard way to select independent columns while
penalizing small determinants. It has uses in numerical linear algebra and
experimental design; [Dereziński--Warmuth, arXiv:1705.06908](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06908)
is a primary example in regression. No downstream regression, ideal-membership
or whole-syzygy-reconstruction speedup is inferred from that context.

The volume-law theorem in Sections 3--5 concerns its exact distribution.
The new Section 5A separately proves a lower bound for finding ANY feasible
pair; that conclusion does not follow from the volume-law lower bound alone.
A request for a larger generating set can still have a different complexity. An explicit coefficient matrix also changes
the input: classical volume sampling is efficient when that matrix is given.

## 2. The nonreduced scheme and the emitted state

1. ASSUME D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE. The row-coisometry promise gives rank(C)=2.
   Hence the tuple spans the two-dimensional quadratic space generated by
   `z0²,z1²`; its ideal is `(z0²,z1²)` and its constant syzygies are ker(C).
   The affine quotient has basis `1,z0,z1,z0z1`, so this is a nonreduced
   length-four scheme supported at the origin. We do not interpret its empty
   projective support as a nonempty projective variety.
2. In the Fock basis `(z0²/sqrt2,z0z1,z1²/sqrt2)` of `R_2`, the coefficient
   matrix F has C as its first and last rows, with zero middle row.
   Thus `F†F=C†C=P_U` and `F F†` is the fixed rank-two projector onto the
   first and last basis vectors. Its operator norm is one.
3. At ambient degree two, `a(f_a)` maps to constants and is the bra of
   the Fock coefficient vector. The success map of the emitter is therefore
   `V=F†`. On the fixed input `1_3/3`, its unnormalized bath output is

       V(1_3/3)V†=P_U/3.

   Its trace is 2/3 and its normalized state is `rho_C=P_U/2`, as promised.
   This also follows from C-369's binomial factor, which is one at N=d.
4. The source model fixes that incident state and discards the other registers.
   Allowing users to choose incident forms, query coefficients, or keep a
   purification would be stronger access and is not part of this theorem.
   The emission-success flag itself is independent of C.
5. Every rank-two subspace U in C^q is realized: choose any isometry W whose
   columns span U and set `C=W†`. Thus the geometry promise includes the
   entire hard source family of D-SUPPORT-BLOCK-PAIR; it does not exclude
   hard supports by restricting the polynomial degree or variable count.

## 3. The target distribution is a syzygy coordinate law

1. For any two labels J, Cauchy--Binet gives

       det(P_U[J,J])=|det C[:,J]|²,
       sum_(|J|=2)|det C[:,J]|²=det(C C†)=1.

   Thus the target is a probability law. Its positive-weight pairs are
   exactly the linearly independent pairs of quadratic forms, and each is
   a minimal generating pair of the ideal.
2. Complete the orthonormal rows of C to a unitary basis using an orthonormal
   column matrix Z for K=ker(C). The complementary-minor identity for a
   unitary matrix equates the absolute determinant of C[:,J] with that of
   Z[J^c,:]. Hence this is equivalently the complementary Pluecker-coordinate
   distribution of the large constant-syzygy space. Global determinant phases
   cancel in the squared modulus; no conjugation operation is performed.
3. The algorithm outputs the two omitted labels, not an exponentially long
   basis for K and not all q-2 complementary labels. This is the exact same
   small classical output required of the comparator.

## 4. Twenty source copies suffice, with an elementary circuit

1. Let `P_-=(1-S)/2` on two copies. Since `rho_C=P_U/2`, its tensor square
   is `P_(U tensor U)/4`. The antisymmetric subspace `wedge² U` is
   one-dimensional. Therefore

       P_- rho_C^(tensor2) P_- = |Omega_U><Omega_U|/4,

   where Omega_U is the normalized exterior state. A swap test accepts with
   probability exactly 1/4 and has that exact conditional output state.
2. Measuring the two source registers and sorting the distinct outcomes gives
   `det(P_U[J,J])`: the two orders of each pair contribute the same half
   probability. An accepted output is always a generating pair.
3. Independent failed trials have probability 3/4. The first-success law is
   the same target on every trial. Ten trials and the fixed-pair fallback
   produce the mixture `(1-f)p_C+f delta_{1,2}`, with

       f=(3/4)^10=0.05631351470947265625 < 1/16.

   Its TV distance from p_C is at most f. The algorithm always outputs and
   consumes at most TWENTY original source copies, including every failure.
4. In the noiseless finite-gate model, the maximum processing cost is ten
   controlled swaps of w-qubit registers, i.e. `10w` Fredkin gates, twenty
   Hadamards, ten one-qubit outcome measurements and final coordinate readout.
   Working quantum storage is at most `2w+1` qubits. Sorting the two labels
   uses O(w) classical work. This is O(log q) processing with a fixed copy cap.
5. The unconditional full protocol is CPTP, including every failed branch and
   fallback. If the noisy delivered input is an INDEPENDENT PRODUCT of source states,
   each within trace distance eps_s of rho_C, and each entire swap-test
   instrument differs by at most eps_t in a
   diamond-distance bound, a conservative hybrid bound adds at most
   `20 eps_s+10 eps_t` to the output TV distance. Thus a sufficient overall
   implementation budget is

       20 eps_s+10 eps_t <= 1/16-(3/4)^10.

   This does not grant arbitrary hardware noise exact support preservation.
   It gives an explicit finite margin for the approximate sampling task.
   Marginal source bounds alone do not control correlated deliveries. With
   correlated inputs one must instead supply a trace-distance bound eps_joint
   on the entire twenty-register state and use eps_joint+10 eps_t.

## 5. Matching the strongest separate-copy comparator

1. ASSUME a classical strategy in D-SYZYGY-PAIR-CLASSICAL-COMPARATOR with
   copy cap T and uniform TV error at most epsilon<1/4. Through step 2.5 it
   is also a projection-DPP sampler on every source in D-SUPPORT-BLOCK-PAIR.
   The source copies and all allowed adaptive POVMs are identical, and the
   output label set and target distribution are identical.
2. Write q=2d. In the two hard support ensembles, the target probability of
   outputting one label from each known coordinate block is respectively
   1 and 1/2. A TV-epsilon sampler therefore separates this event by at least
   `1/2-2epsilon` after ensemble averaging.
3. The independently proved full-transcript bound C-362 gives

       TV(P_0,P_1) <= T(T-1)/d.

   It permits arbitrary global measurements within each original copy,
   adaptive choices, unlimited classical memory/computation and all failures.
   Applying data processing to the output event yields

       T(T-1) >= d(1/2-2epsilon).

   At epsilon=1/16 this is `T(T-1)>=3q/16`, hence Omega(sqrt(q)) source
   copies. The strict cap of twenty is smaller than this lower bound for
   every even q>=2028; asymptotically the separation is constant versus
   square-root dimension, or exponential in the register width w.
4. This is an unconditional theorem in the stated quantum-data model. It is
   not based on a complexity-class conjecture, a dense-versus-sparse comparison,
   a quantum-state-versus-tomography output mismatch, or classical random walks.
   It proves a COPY advantage over the specified separate-copy strategies.
   Giving classical coefficients, a controlled emitter or quantum memory
   across copies would change the task and is not covered by the lower bound.

The detailed lower-bound proof is retained in
`scouting/support-plucker-classical-r9.md`, with independent review in
`verdicts/support-plucker-r9.md` sections 3.4--3.5. This memo transfers that

## 5A. Eight copies suffice for ANY minimal generating pair

This strengthens the useful output and requires a new lower-bound argument.
A sampler lower bound alone would not rule out easier unweighted selection.

### 5A.1 Quantum upper

Every successful antisymmetric trial in Section 4 returns an independent pair
of original generators. Four independent trials therefore give a correct pair
with probability at least

    1-(3/4)^4=175/256 > 2/3,

using at most EIGHT original copies. The fixed fallback may also be correct;
the bound does not count on that. Processing requires at most `4w` Fredkin
gates, eight Hadamards and `2w+1` live qubits, plus source and output costs.
No determinant value, state reconstruction or membership oracle is needed.
For independent product source errors and instrument errors, a sufficient
robustness condition is `8 eps_s+4 eps_t <=13/768`; a joint eight-register
source error bound can replace `8 eps_s`. This is the exact margin between
175/256 and 2/3. Marginal-only bounds on correlated source copies do not suffice.

### 5A.2 A hidden-partition family

Take q=2d with d>=3. Draw a uniformly random labelled subset S of size d,
then independent Haar unit vectors u on coordinates S and v on its complement.
Keep this partition and both vectors fixed throughout the algorithm. Set
`U=span(u,v)` and `C=[u,v]†`. Almost surely all coordinates inside each support
are nonzero. A selected pair generates the full quadratic ideal if and only if
it has one index in S and one in its complement.

The partition is NOT public metadata. It specifies which original generators
are scalar multiples of g0 and which are scalar multiples of g1, and finding
indices of different types is precisely the desired basis-selection task.
The realized source is still exactly P_U/2 and obeys every canonical promise.

### 5A.3 Uniform transcript minorization and success bound

Fix any adaptive separate-copy strategy with total copy cap T. For EACH fixed
S, the theta=0 argument in C-362, after permuting coordinate blocks, gives

    P_S >= c_T P_*,   c_T=(d/(d+T-1))^T,
    1-c_T <= T(T-1)/d.

Here P_S is averaged over the two Haar vectors with that fixed partition.
Crucially, P_* is the transcript law of this SAME strategy on independent
maximally mixed states `1_q/q`. It is independent of S. The underlying
Haar/Gaussian polynomial comparison is uniform over all measurement leaves;
it is not limited to a strategy designed for a particular public partition.
S being absent from the algorithm's input is essential to this common reference.

The joint law of the hidden partition and transcript therefore has a common
component of mass c_T in which the transcript, and hence the output pair, is
independent of S. Any fixed distinct pair crosses a uniformly random balanced
partition with probability

    a0=2 binom(2d-2,d-1)/binom(2d,d)=d/(2d-1).

On the remaining component, success is at most one. Thus average success is at
most `c_T a0+(1-c_T)`. Uniform success at least 2/3 on every promised C implies
average success at least 2/3, so

    1-c_T >= (d-2)/[3(d-1)],
    T(T-1) >= d(d-2)/[3(d-1)].

This is `T=Omega(sqrt(q))`, not Omega(q). A total cap of eight violates the
bound at d=170 and above, i.e. every even q>=340. It is a worst-case
unconditional copy-complexity separation. It does not claim an expected-stopping
lower bound, a public-partition lower bound, or an explicit-coefficient-list bound.

### 5A.4 Interpretation

The new task simply selects a minimal subset of the original available
constraints. Volume weighting is a property of the quantum construction,
not an artificial restriction imposed on the classical output. The hard
family proves that separate-copy measurements cannot achieve the same
success guarantee with a dimension-independent fixed copy cap.

The bare ideal remains known and fixed. Asking for any abstract generating
forms would be trivial; asking which ORIGINAL labelled generators to retain
is a different based-presentation reduction problem. The theorem states that
problem explicitly and does not turn it into an intrinsic new invariant.

## 5B. A concrete separate-copy upper bound

The lower bound covers every allowed classical strategy, so the advantage does
not depend on choosing a weak implementation as the comparator. An explicit
reconstruction-based upper nevertheless makes the remaining classical gap clear.
The construction below uses O(q log q) separate copies at fixed accuracy; it
is a sufficient upper, not a claim that tomography is necessary or optimal for
this smaller generator-selection task.

Pad the register to `D=2^ceil(log2 q)<2q`. On each source copy, independently
choose a uniformly random Clifford basis and measure it. If its observed
basis vector is phi, form the classical matrix

    X=(D+1)|phi><phi|-1_D.

The Clifford basis ensemble is a projective two-design. Its exact second
moment gives

    E X=rho_C,
    E X²=(D-1)rho_C+D 1_D,
    ||E (X-rho_C)²||<=3D/2,   ||X-rho_C||<=D+1.

For the sample mean rho_hat, matrix Bernstein therefore gives operator error
at most a with failure at most delta when

    N >= [3D+2(D+1)a/3] a^(-2) log(2D/delta).

Take `a=1/32`, `delta=1/12`, and take the top two eigenvectors of rho_hat.
Because the true nonzero eigenvalues are 1/2, the resulting support projector
P_hat satisfies `||(1-P_U)P_hat||<=a/(1/2-a)=1/15`. This follows directly
by restricting `(1-P_U)rho_hat P_hat=(1-P_U)(rho_hat-rho_C)P_hat` to its
estimated eigenspace, whose eigenvalues are at least `1/2-a`.

The exterior rays of the two supports have trace distance at most
`sqrt(2)/15`: their squared overlap is the product of squared principal-angle
cosines. Classically sample the rank-two projection DPP of P_hat, using its
explicit two-column frame and the sequential conditional formula. Its TV
error is at most that exterior-ray distance. Map padded or invalid labels
to a fixed original pair. The resulting original-generator success is at
least `(11/12)(1-sqrt(2)/15)>2/3`.

Thus an O(q log q)-copy, polynomial classical processing upper is available;
our proven lower is Omega(sqrt q). Closing that gap for the any-pair task is
open here. This neither weakens the eight-copy quantum theorem nor claims
that every classical algorithm reconstructs the source. Per-copy Clifford
measurements have polynomial cost in log q; forming and diagonalizing the
classical estimator has polynomial cost and storage in q.

Primary ingredients checked after deriving this baseline:
[Dankert--Cleve--Emerson--Livine, quant-ph/0606161](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0606161)
for Clifford two-designs and
[Tropp, arXiv:1004.4389](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4389) for matrix Bernstein.
The task-specific eigenvector and generating-pair argument is given above.

## 6. Source and hardware costs

For the principal any-pair task, the cap is eight delivered states and
marginal quantum cost is `8 C_source(q)+O(log q)`. Twenty-four raw fixed-emitter
calls provide eight successes except with probability at most `exp(-16/3)`;
combining this with `(3/4)^4` gives total failure below 1/3. These raw-call and
delivered-copy bounds are distinct. The same source-only lower bound applies
when C-independent failed emissions are counted, since they reveal no data.

For the additional volume-law task, the copy bound charges twenty delivered source states. With per-copy delivery
cost C_source(q), quantum marginal cost is at most `20 C_source(q)+O(log q)`;
the classical copy lower bound multiplies the same source cost. A one-time
device construction cost B_source(q) is common and may dominate both totals.
No claim about loading a freely available coefficient matrix follows.

If raw fixed-emitter calls are the counted primitive, fifty calls suffice with
room in the same TV budget: fewer than twenty successes has probability at
most `exp(-64/9)` by Hoeffding, since each succeeds with probability 2/3.
Abort to the fixed pair if that event occurs. The total ideal TV error is at
most `(3/4)^10+exp(-64/9)<1/16`. Failure flags reveal no C-dependent information.
This raw-call statement is separate from the delivered-copy cap, and its
finite implementation has the correspondingly smaller remaining error budget.

A small natural optical diagnostic needs TWO photons per trial. Each photon's
internal q-mode state is rho_C; the two external input paths interfere at a
balanced beam splitter. A coincidence with one photon in each output path
implements the antisymmetric projection on the internal states. Resolve the
internal mode in each detector and sort those labels. Only the classical pair
is wanted, so destructive detection is enough; no nondemolition state output
or many-photon cat state is required.

For q=4, use four matched internal modes in each external path and mode-resolved
coincidence detection (at most eight output channels). The ideal coincidence
rate is 1/4, and the six sorted-pair probabilities should match the six minors
of C. Vary a known two-dimensional source support to compare the measured law
with its exact classical calibration. Four trials implement the capped any-generating-pair algorithm; ten implement
the additional volume-law sampler.
This tests the quantum instrument and geometric probability law, not a practical
asymptotic advantage at q=4. Static mode count, source preparation, optical
loss, distinguishability and detector bias are measured physical resources.
Uniform independent loss lowers the accepted rate and changes required trials;
mode-dependent loss can also change the law and needs separate calibration.

The optical swap-test/Hong--Ou--Mandel correspondence is known. It is an
eligible mechanism under D25, not a claim of inventing two-photon interference.
The primary operation comparison is
[García-Escartín--Chamorro-Posada, arXiv:1303.6814](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.6814),
DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.87.052330. The coincidence calculation here additionally
keeps the resolved generator labels that define the required volume law.

### 6A. Why fixed-source access is an essential premise

A controllable emitter gives a DIFFERENT and easier problem. Suppose a
classical controller may prepare any known pure incident state in span(g0,g1),
query the isometry C†, and measure the emitted label, without retaining quantum
memory between queries. Choose one of the three Pauli eigenbases uniformly, with a random order
(x,x_perp), and make one query for each incident qubit state. This finite
ensemble has the same second moment as a Haar-random orthonormal pair. These queries succeed with
probability one, since the emitter is isometric on that two-dimensional space.

Write c_i for column i of C and w_i=||c_i||². The expected ordered-pair
probability in this experiment is

    E_x |c_i†x|² |c_j†x_perp|²
      =[2 w_i w_j-|c_i†c_j|²]/6.

Partition the nonzero columns into projective parallel classes. A class with
unit direction v and total weight m contributes `m vv†<=CC†=1`, hence m<=1;
the class weights sum to two. A dependent pair comes from one such class,
so its total probability is `sum_classes m²/6<=1/3`. This two-query controller
therefore finds a generating pair with probability at least 2/3 for every C.

This does not refute the eight-copy theorem: its incident source is fixed,
maximally mixed and inaccessible. It demonstrates why changing that interface
invalidates the lower bound, even without a coefficient list. A practical
application must genuinely supply bath copies or an otherwise fixed quantum
source; if the relevant incident-state controls are available at comparable
cost, this classical-control baseline is the appropriate comparison.

## 7. What this does and does not establish under D25

The principal computational task is minimal original-generator selection
through a quantum syzygy Gram source; the volume-law sampler is an additional
output guarantee. The scheme, source and output maps are explicit;
the quantum copy cap and full adaptive classical lower bound are matched;
the processing uses swap interference without QFT/Grover/DQI; and a two-photon
experiment can test a small member of the construction. These are submitted
for independent judgment against all current PRD criteria.

Its scope is a formal quantum-data advantage. Classical coefficient-list
syzygy computation, useful speedups for arbitrary ideal generators, a new
intrinsic invariant of the fixed scheme, and practical large-scale hardware
advantage are not established. The separate BosonSampling candidate addresses
a compact classical input with different complexity assumptions and should
be assessed separately rather than inheriting this unconditional copy bound.
