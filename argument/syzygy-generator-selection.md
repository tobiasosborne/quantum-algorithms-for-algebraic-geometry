# Eight-copy selection of original generators

2026-09-08. A finite-source theorem under PRD D25. Canonical definitions are
in `definitions/syzygy-plucker-r12.md`; independent approval is in
`verdicts/syzygy-advantage-r12.md`. This argument concerns selecting labels
from a based tuple, not computing an abstract basis of the known fixed ideal.

## Theorem

ASSUME D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE and D-SYZYGY-PAIR-SOURCE. PROVE that the
D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-OUTPUT task has a quantum algorithm with at most eight
source copies, success at least 175/256 and O(log q) processing gates. For
q=2d,d>=3, every D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-COMPARATOR with success at least 2/3
on every promised tuple satisfies

    T(T-1) >= d(d-2)/[3(d-1)].

The gap is an unconditional fixed-copy-cap separation in that source model.

### 1. Identify the geometry and source

1.1. ASSUME the hidden matrix C from D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE. PROVE its rank is
two and ker(C) is exactly the constant-coefficient relation space of the tuple.
The equation `CC†=1_2` gives rank two, and linear independence of the two
Fock basis quadrics makes `sum v_a f_a=0` equivalent to `Cv=0`.
REFERENCE: D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE, elementary linear independence.

1.2. PROVE any two original labels J generate the full ideal iff
`det C[:,J]!=0`. The full tuple spans g0,g1 by 1.1; a two-column invertible
matrix spans them, whereas a singular pair spans at most one quadratic.
An ideal generated by one homogeneous quadratic cannot contain the full
two-dimensional degree-two generator space. REFERENCE: 1.1 and homogeneous
degree-two comparison in D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE.

1.3. PROVE the source realization gives `rho_C=C†C/2`, success 2/3.
In the ordered three-dimensional Fock basis the emitter has matrix `V=F†`,
where F has rows C_0,0,C_1. Thus `V(1_3/3)V†=C†C/3`; its trace is 2/3.
Conjugated annihilation fixes the adjoint orientation. REFERENCE:
D-THERMAL-SYZYGY-EMITTER, C3, and D-SYZYGY-PAIR-SOURCE.

1.4. PROVE every rank-two flat source is included. For any orthonormal
two-frame W, choose `C=W†`. Then `CC†=1_2` and `C†C=WW†`. REFERENCE:
D-SYZYGY-PAIR-TUPLE and D-SUPPORT-BLOCK-PAIR.

### 2. Construct the quantum instrument

2.1. PROVE the antisymmetric swap outcome on two copies has probability
1/4 and conditional state Omega_U. On U tensor U the state is `1_4/4`;
the antisymmetric projector has rank one there. Hence
`P_- rho_C^(tensor2) P_-=|Omega_U><Omega_U|/4`.
REFERENCE: D-SYZYGY-PAIR-SWAP-PROTOCOL and D-SUPPORT-PLUCKER-OUTPUT.

2.2. PROVE every coordinate pair produced by an accepted trial generates.
The sorted coordinate probability in Omega_U is
`|det C[:,J]|²`, so every possible accepted pair has nonzero determinant.
REFERENCE: 2.1, Cauchy--Binet and 1.2.

2.3. PROVE the fixed-cap bound. Four independent trials fail with probability
`(3/4)^4`; the fallback is allowed to be wrong. Therefore success is at
least `1-(3/4)^4=175/256>2/3`, with eight copies including all failures.
REFERENCE: 2.1--2.2 and D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-PROTOCOL.

2.4. PROVE the processing cost and mechanism scope. Each standard swap test
uses one control ancilla, two Hadamards and w controlled swaps of corresponding
data qubits. Thus four trials use at most `4w` Fredkin gates and eight
Hadamards, with `2w+1` live qubits. No controlled source preparation or input
inverse occurs. REFERENCE: D-SYZYGY-PAIR-SWAP-PROTOCOL. The accepted effect
is direct antisymmetric comparison, not QFT, Grover or DQI processing; this
is eligible under D25.

### 3. Define a distribution of fixed hard inputs

3.1. ASSUME q=2d,d>=3. Draw S uniformly among d-element subsets, then draw
independent Haar unit vectors u on S and v on its complement. Both vectors
and S remain fixed for the entire algorithm. Set `C=[u,v]†`.
PROVE this is a legal source by 1.4. REFERENCE:
D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-COMPARATOR and D-SUPPORT-BLOCK-PAIR, theta-zero family
after a coordinate permutation.

3.2. PROVE a returned pair is generating iff it crosses S, almost surely
over the fixed Haar draw. Within either part, all generator columns lie on
one of the two independent coordinate axes; Haar coordinates are nonzero
almost surely. REFERENCE: 3.1, 1.2 and absolute continuity of Haar vectors.

### 4. Keep the reference transcript independent of the hidden partition

4.1. Fix one allowed adaptive algorithm, including its classical randomness
and its fixed copy cap T. For every fixed S, apply C-362's theta-zero
minorization proof to that SAME algorithm, with its matrices permuted into
the two blocks. This yields

    P_S >= c_T P_*,   c_T=(d/(d+T-1))^T,
    1-c_T <= T(T-1)/d.

P_S is averaged over u,v for that fixed partition. REFERENCE: C-362 and
`scouting/support-plucker-classical-r9.md` complete adaptive-transcript proof.

4.2. PROVE that P_* is the same for all S. In the cited proof it is the
distribution of the fixed algorithm on independent `1_q/q` copies. The
Gaussian one-copy covariance is `1_q/q` for every balanced partition, and
the algorithm does not receive S as metadata. Thus the reference protocol
and all its conditional measurement choices depend on its transcript, not S.
REFERENCE: 4.1 and the input restrictions of D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-COMPARATOR.

4.3. PROVE the joint hidden-input/transcript distribution contains a
partition-independent component of mass c_T. Multiply the measure inequality
in 4.1 by the uniform prior on S and use 4.2. The remaining nonnegative
measure has mass `1-c_T`. No statement about individual unaveraged source
states is needed. REFERENCE: 4.1--4.2, elementary measure decomposition.

### 5. Bound any classical output strategy

5.1. PROVE any fixed distinct pair crosses a uniformly random balanced S
with probability `a0=d/(2d-1)`. Count the partitions containing exactly one
of its two elements: `2 binom(2d-2,d-1)/binom(2d,d)`.
REFERENCE: 3.1 and elementary counting.

5.2. On the common component of 4.3, the output is independent of S.
By 5.1 its success is at most a0; on the remaining component it is at most
one. Therefore average success is at most `c_T a0+(1-c_T)`.
REFERENCE: 3.2, 4.3 and 5.1. Invalid outputs or failures cannot improve this bound.

5.3. Uniform success at least 2/3 for every promised C implies average
success at least 2/3 on 3.1. Rearranging 5.2 yields
`1-c_T >= (d-2)/[3(d-1)]`; using 4.1 proves the theorem's lower bound.
REFERENCE: 5.2, 4.1 and the uniform output promise.

5.4. At d=170 the right side is `28560/507>56=8*7`, and it increases with
d>=170. Therefore the quantum cap eight is strictly below every classical
cap for even q>=340; the general asymptotic lower is Omega(sqrt q).
REFERENCE: 5.3 and 2.3. No expected-stopping or coefficient-list lower bound
is inferred from this fixed-cap theorem.

### 6. Physical and usefulness scope

6.1. A balanced two-photon interferometer with resolved internal modes gives
the same antisymmetric coincidence effect; small q tests the pair law and
the success rate. REFERENCE: `scouting/syzygy-plucker-r12.md` section 6 and
García-Escartín--Chamorro-Posada, arXiv:1303.6814. This supplies the requested
small hardware route, not evidence of practical asymptotic optical advantage.

6.2. The useful output selects a minimal subset of ORIGINAL available
constraint channels. The bare ideal and an abstract generating pair are known,
so returning g0,g1 instead would solve a different trivial task. Copy delivery,
device startup, optical mode count and errors have the explicit scope in the
canonical source definition and scouting section 6. REFERENCE:
D-SYZYGY-GENERATOR-OUTPUT and D-SYZYGY-PAIR-SOURCE.

6.3. The finite checker verifies matrix identities and combinatorial constants;
it does not replace steps 4--5. REFERENCE:
`checkers/explore/syzygy_plucker_r12.py`, 1032 finite checks and six red mutations.
