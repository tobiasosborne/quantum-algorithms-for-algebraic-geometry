01

A real advance. An unfinished goal.

The project has proved quantum advantages in the number of copies needed for two precisely matched tasks. It has not yet found a candidate that meets its central demand: an original quantum mechanism solving a new algebraic-geometric problem with a demonstrated advantage.

The distinction matters. The positive theorems are substantial mathematical results. Their displayed mechanisms reduce to operations already described in quantum information, so they do not satisfy the project’s strict originality criterion, D22.

The search began with a useful bridge: multiplication by a polynomial becomes a matrix, and polynomial constraints become a positive quantum Hamiltonian. It then widened into real geometry, robotics, topology, tensor decomposition, representation theory, and quantum data. The report follows both the successful constructions and the tests that changed their interpretation.

1. A mathematical statement

A claimed identity or bound needs a proof under explicit assumptions. This can survive even when the proposed algorithm fails.

2. An algorithmic advantage

Quantum and classical procedures must receive comparable inputs and produce the same output. Every preparation, failure, and repetition is charged.

3. An original mechanism

A new geometric use of a known quantum procedure does not meet this project’s goal. Historical originality is a separate audit.

What would count as a completed discovery?

The PRD requires all six conditions together:

  1. 01A precise geometric problem and input encoding.
  2. 02A checked best classical baseline on the same parameters.
  3. 03A proved quantum resource advantage.
  4. 04An audit of hidden costs and classical simulation attacks.
  5. 05A concrete, inexpensive experimental route.
  6. 06Originality of both the problem-level result and the quantum mechanism.

The quantum-copy theorems settle narrower, formal questions. Neither a proof status nor an attractive small circuit automatically settles this six-part test.

02

From equations to a Hilbert space

You already know the ingredients: vectors, inner products, linear maps, and kernels. Algebraic geometry adds a way to organize equations and their common solutions.

A variety is a set cut out by polynomial equations. A circle is the real zero set of \(x^2+y^2-1\). Two equations ask for common zeros. The picture below is a real slice; most of the project’s foundational construction lives over complex numbers and in projective space.

Equations as a closed collection
Ideal

If \(f_1,\ldots,f_m\) vanish, so does any polynomial combination \(\sum_i g_i f_i\). The ideal is the collection of all these consequences, not just the original list of equations.

One total degree at a time
Homogeneous polynomial

Every monomial has the same total degree: \(x^2+xy+y^2\), for example. A nonhomogeneous equation can often be homogenized with an extra coordinate, bringing projective geometry into view.

A finite-dimensional vector space
Degree-N coefficient space

Fix the degree. The monomials become basis vectors, and polynomial coefficients become a vector. With \(n+1\) variables this space has dimension \(\binom{N+n}{n}\). An inner-product convention determines its normalized basis.

Polynomial arithmetic becomes linear algebra
Multiplication map

Multiplying an unknown degree-\(N-d\) polynomial by a fixed degree-\(d\) polynomial is a linear map \(M_f\). Stack these maps for all generators: the range is the degree-\(N\) piece of the ideal.

A quantum nullspace
Dark state

A vector annihilated by every \(M_{f_i}^{\dagger}\) has zero energy under \(\sum_iM_{f_i}M_{f_i}^{\dagger}\). This space is the orthogonal complement of the ideal’s degree piece.

Dimension, not a list of roots
Hilbert function

The dimension of this complement counts independent degree-\(N\) classes modulo the ideal. It is useful geometric information; it does not generally equal the number of points or reveal their coordinates.

The coherent-state dictionary adds a physical interpretation. Under the chosen conjugation and Fock-basis conventions, evaluation at a point is represented by an inner product with a symmetric tensor. A common zero therefore supplies a special dark vector. General dark vectors can be superpositions and need not encode individual geometric points.

03

The Hamiltonian is only the beginning

The seed construction packages the multiplication maps into a positive operator. A state has small energy when it nearly satisfies all the adjoint constraints.

\[H_N=\sum_iM_{f_i,N}M_{f_i,N}^{\dagger},\qquad\ker H_N=(I_N)^{\perp}.\]

The adjoints and singular values depend on the declared inner product. This report uses the orthonormal Fock convention for the explicit example below.

Turning this identity into an efficient algorithm demands more. You need a way to implement the operator, a sufficiently large normalized spectral gap, and enough overlap between the prepared input and the subspace you want to measure.

The overlap bill comes separately

Even a well-separated zero-energy subspace can be almost invisible to your starting state. A maximally mixed probe lands in a dark space of dimension \(h_N\) with probability \(h_N/M_N\). When that fraction is exponentially small, a clean spectral theorem has not made the desired event easy to observe.

Several routes failed at this point or at the comparator. Dense matrix diagonalization is usually the wrong classical baseline: the multiplication operator can be sparse and implicit, inviting Krylov methods, iterative least squares, and stochastic trace estimation. A compact quantum register alone also does not prove a space advantage over a classical algorithm that rereads the input.

04

The input model is part of the theorem

A list of coefficients and copies of a coefficient state carry the same symbolic amplitudes in very different ways. They are not interchangeable resources.

The positive copy separations compare two procedures receiving the same quantum source. The coherent procedure can jointly process multiple original copies. The separate-copy comparator can make an arbitrary global POVM inside each complete original copy, choose future measurements adaptively, and keep unlimited classical memory. It cannot retain quantum memory between original copies.

Postselection consumes the failures too

A successful branch can look cheap while its source bill grows rapidly. Suppose a displayed recursive filter needs two independently prepared children at each level and succeeds with probability \(p\). If every failure forces fresh children, its expected source cost obeys \(C_t=(2/p)^t\). This is the cost of that recursion, not a universal lower bound on every possible algorithm.

05

Positive result I: sample a hidden subspace

A mixed quantum state hides a subspace in its support. Joint measurements can extract the subspace’s oriented-volume state—and then sample a classical coordinate subset—using fewer source copies than any separate-copy strategy.

The source supplies independent copies of a rank-\(r\) density operator \(\rho\) on \(\mathbb C^q\), with each nonzero eigenvalue at least \(\eta/r\). Write \(U=\operatorname{range}\rho\). No basis for \(U\) is supplied. The desired state is the normalized antisymmetric tensor of an orthonormal basis of \(U\):

\[|\Omega_U\rangle=u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_r.\]

Changing the support basis changes only a global phase. This is the support Slater state, representing the Plücker coordinates of the subspace.

Measure its registers in the coordinate basis and sort the outcomes. The result is an \(r\)-element subset \(J\) with probability \(\det(P_U[J,J])\), a projection determinantal point process (DPP). This gives a classical-output task for which both algorithms can be compared directly. It is a DPP of the support projector, not of the density operator \(\rho\).

A matched classical-output copy advantage

The broader construction uses measured tableau steering: collective permutation measurements locate a suitable symmetry sector and steer it toward an antisymmetric column. A final exact certificate ensures that every accepted output is the desired state despite finite-precision steering. Its source budget is \(O((r^2/\eta)\log(1/\delta))\), and its circuit size is polynomial in the support parameters and logarithmic in ambient dimension, with conservative exponents.

For flat rank two, the small protocol gives a much cleaner upper bound. Repeat independent three-copy trials a capped number of times and use a fixed coordinate pair if all fail. The fallback makes the sampler always output; its total-variation error is at most its failure probability.

The displayed conversion mechanism is built from known Schur–Weyl measurements, quantum rewinding / alternating projections, and antisymmetrization, so its claim to meet D22 is refuted. The general gate bound is an existence proof, and arbitrary physical noise is outside its exact-support promise.

06

Positive result II: test hidden components

A second result asks a geometric question about components that are not supplied individually. A coherent measurement can answer that question without reconstructing the component list.

The source is a normalized symmetric tensor with four independent components,

\[T=\sum_{a=1}^{4}c_a\,u_a^{\otimes4},\qquad u_a\in\mathbb C^d_A\otimes\mathbb C^d_B,\quad q=d^2,\ d\geq20.\]

The promise includes component Gram matrix \(G\succeq\tfrac12 I\) and determinant-seed probability \(p_0\geq1/512\). The components and their coefficients are unknown.

This is a Waring decomposition: a homogeneous symmetric tensor expressed as a sum of tensor powers. The task concerns the unique component points, not the rank of the full source across an arbitrary cut.

Each component is bipartite. Let its Schmidt probabilities be the eigenvalues of \(\operatorname{Tr}_B|u_a\rangle\langle u_a|\). The fifth elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_5\) sums all products of five distinct probabilities. It vanishes exactly when at most four Schmidt probabilities are nonzero.

\[\Gamma=\frac14\sum_{a=1}^{4}e_5\!\left(\operatorname{Tr}_B|u_a\rangle\langle u_a|\right).\]

Decide the promised alternatives: \(\Gamma=0\) versus \(\Gamma\geq10^{-6}\).

The important unit is the original source copy. Each original \(T\) already contains four component slots. A coherent processing branch can create an internal six-slot component row; measuring five A registers from that row still costs five original source copies per trial. The classical lower bound allows arbitrary joint operations within a single original \(T\), so it does not mistake its internal slots for separate classical inputs.

The direct event has probability at least \((9/2^{31})\Gamma\), and exactly zero under the \(\Gamma=0\) promise. Independent repetition therefore gives a dimension-independent sufficient copy budget for constant decision error.

The lower-bound construction hides moment-matched component spectra in random local frames. Low-copy transcripts cannot reliably recover the bit even though a joint event can expose the determinantal condition. Independently, five original copies are minimal among nontrivial batches with exact perfect completeness; that scoped statement does not establish total-copy optimality or optimality with two-sided error.

Mathematics and novelty receive different verdicts. The same-bit-output separation and the direct tester are proved. The displayed mechanism uses known universal comparison and programmable-discrimination operations, so it does not fulfill D22. A generic rank test on the entire source is not asserted to solve the full hidden-component promise.

08

Make every conclusion inspectable

The campaign separates proposing an idea from checking it. The claim registry preserves the distinction between a proof, a sketch, a conjecture, and a refuted assertion—including refuted novelty claims whose underlying mathematics survives.

Independent reviews attack assumptions, normalization, access models, and comparison algorithms. Finite numerical probes help find mistakes and test special cases. Deliberately wrong variants check whether a numerical checker can actually detect the errors it is meant to catch. Neither an agreeing plot nor many passing tests replaces a proof.

09

What remains worth asking

The surviving question is not whether geometry can be written in quantum notation. It is whether a concrete geometric task forces a useful quantum operation whose advantage, input costs, and originality all withstand scrutiny.

A negative result can sharpen that search. The disk family pinpoints an information-access obstruction. Classical sketching and sampling attacks identify outputs that were easier than they looked. Exact geometric identities remain available as instruments, even when the original claim of acceleration is withdrawn.
