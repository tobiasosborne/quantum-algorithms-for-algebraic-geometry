# Projective varieties as ground spaces: what the construction is, what it can compute, and realistic conjectures

Analysis of the handwritten notes `page81.tex` (complex projective varieties and quantum mechanics). Sections 1–3 fix the setting and identify the construction; 4–7 assess the quantum-algorithmic content; 8 collects the conjectures, separated from what is already a theorem; 9 lists corrections to the notes. Everything marked *numerically* was checked by exact diagonalisation (scripts in the session scratchpad, `numerics/`); everything in Section 8 survived one adversarial referee pass, which killed or weakened about a third of the first draft's claims.

---

## 1. Setting, made precise

Let $R=\mathbb{C}[z_0,\dots,z_n]=\bigoplus_N R_N$ with $R_N$ the homogeneous polynomials of degree $N$, $\dim R_N=\binom{N+n}{n}$. Three inner products appear in this business.

| name | $\|z^k\|^2$ | adjoint of $z_j$ |
|---|---|---|
| Fock / Fischer (Gaussian measure on $\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$) | $k!:=\prod_j k_j!$ | $\partial_j$ exactly |
| Bombieri–Weyl / Kostlan / Drury–Arveson | $k!/N!$ | $\partial_j$ up to a degree-dependent scalar |
| sphere $S^{2n+1}$ (the notes' normalisation) | $n!\,k!/(N+n)!$ | $\partial_j\,(D+n)^{-1}$, $D$ = degree operator |

Within a fixed $R_N$ these differ by a scalar, so orthogonal complements, kernels of homogeneous differential operators and the ground spaces below coincide in all three. They are *not* the same Hilbert module across degrees: norms of the graded direct sums, boundedness of the shifts, and everything in the Drury–Arveson dictionary (Section 2) depend on which one is used. I use the Fock form throughout: $|k\rangle=z^k/\sqrt{k!}$ is orthonormal, $a_j=\partial_j$, $a_j^\dagger=z_j$, $[a_i,a_j^\dagger]=\delta_{ij}$. Everything is then literally $n+1$ bosonic modes, and $R_N$ is the $N$-boson sector,

$$
R_N\;\cong\;\mathrm{Sym}^N(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})\;=\;H^0(\mathbb{P}^n,\mathcal{O}(N)),
$$

the symmetric subspace of $N$ qudits of dimension $n+1$ (not $n$), i.e. the ambient space of the $N$-th Veronese embedding. The notes' $L^2(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ should be read as the Hardy space of the sphere or as symmetric Fock space; the genuine $L^2(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ is a different, non-holomorphic space.

For a homogeneous $f=\sum_\alpha f_\alpha z^\alpha$ of degree $m$ put

$$
a^\dagger(f)=f(a^\dagger)=\text{multiplication by }f,\qquad
a(f):=a^\dagger(f)^\dagger=\bar f(\partial)=\sum_\alpha\overline{f_\alpha}\,\partial^\alpha .
$$

The conjugation on the coefficients is forced by self-adjointness; the notes write $f(\partial)$, which agrees only for real $f$.

## 2. The ground space is Macaulay's inverse system (this part of the notes is correct and classical)

For $I=(f_1,\dots,f_d)$ homogeneous with $\deg f_j=m_j$, $I_N=\sum_jf_jR_{N-m_j}$, define

$$
H=\sum_{j=1}^da^\dagger(f_j)\,a(f_j),\qquad H_N:=H|_{R_N}.
$$

**Fact 2.1 (frustration-free; kernel = orthogonal complement of the ideal).** Since $\ker M^\dagger=(\operatorname{ran}M)^\perp$,

$$
\ker H_N=\bigcap_j\ker a(f_j)|_{R_N}=\bigcap_j(f_jR_{N-m_j})^\perp=(I_N)^\perp,\qquad
\dim\ker H_N=\binom{N+n}{n}-\dim I_N=\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(N).
$$

The ground-state degeneracy of $H_N$ is the Hilbert function of $R/I$; for $N$ beyond the regularity it is the Hilbert polynomial, whose degree is $\dim V(I)$ and whose leading coefficient is $\deg V(I)/(\dim V)!$. The range of $H_N$ is $I_N$ itself, so the projector onto $I_N$ is the step function $\Theta(H_N)$. *Numerically:* $\dim\ker H_N$ agreed with an independent exact rank of the Macaulay matrix for the conic ($2N+1$), the twisted cubic ($3N+1$), the rational normal quartic ($4N+1$), a monomial ideal, two generic quadrics in $\mathbb P^3$ ($4N$) and the boolean ideals of Section 6, and $\operatorname{span}\{z^\beta f_j\}\perp\ker H_N$ held to $10^{-14}$.

**Fact 2.2 (identification).** $(I_N)^\perp=\{q\in R_N:\bar f(\partial)q=0\ \forall f\in I\}$ is the degree-$N$ part of the *Macaulay inverse system* of $\bar I$ (apolarity; Macaulay 1916, Iarrobino–Kanev 1999), and for a principal ideal $R_N=fR_{N-m}\oplus\ker\bar f(\partial)$ is Fischer's decomposition (Fischer 1918; Shapiro 1989). For general $I$ the orthogonal decomposition $R_N=I_N\oplus I_N^\perp$ is elementary, but the identification of the complement with the inverse system is the useful content. The map $(R/I)_N\to(I_N)^\perp$, $[q]\mapsto P_0q$, is an isomorphism, so the ground space *is* the graded quotient ring as a vector space; its multiplicative structure is the compression of the creation operators, $P_{0,N+1}\,a_j^\dagger\,P_{0,N}=$ multiplication by $z_j$ on $(R/I)_N\to(R/I)_{N+1}$.

**Operator-theoretic dictionary.** With the Bombieri–Weyl norm the graded sum $\bigoplus_NR_N$ is the Drury–Arveson space $H^2_{n+1}$, Arveson's $d$-shift is $S_j=a_j^\dagger/\sqrt{\hat N+1}$ (the creation operator normalised to a contraction), the closure of $I$ is a graded submodule, and $\bigoplus_N(I_N)^\perp$ is the quotient module $H^2_{n+1}/[I]$ on which the compressed $S_j$ act. Arveson's curvature invariant equals the Euler characteristic for graded modules (Arveson 2000) and, by Fang (2003), is the leading multiplicity of the Hilbert polynomial of $R/I$; the dimension of the module is $\deg p_{R/I}+1$. The Arveson–Douglas conjecture (essential normality of these compressed shifts, Schatten class $p>\dim$ of the affine cone) is proved for principal, monomial, one-dimensional and radical-linear-union cases and for varieties smooth away from the origin (Engliš–Eschmeier 2015; Douglas–Tang–Yu 2016), and open in general as of 2025. None of this is new, but it is exactly the mathematics of the ground spaces here, and the notes' objects have standard names there.

**Fact 2.3 (the notes' worked example).** For $f=z_0z_1-z_2^2$: $a(f)z_0z_1z_2^2=z_2^2-2z_0z_1$ and $a(f)(z_0z_1+cz_2^2)=1-2c$, so $c=1/2$, both as in the notes. The line "$1-2\overset{?}{=}-1$" has a sign slip: $a(f)f=\partial_0\partial_1(z_0z_1)+\partial_2^2(z_2^2)=1+2=3=\|f\|^2$. In degree 2, $\dim\ker H_2=5=2\cdot2+1$.

## 3. What kind of "local" Hamiltonian this is

Under $R_N\cong\mathrm{Sym}^N(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})$ (unitarily, $|k\rangle\mapsto$ the normalised symmetrisation of $|0\rangle^{\otimes k_0}\cdots|n\rangle^{\otimes k_n}$), a degree-$m$ form $f$ corresponds to the symmetric tensor

$$
|F\rangle=\sum_\alpha f_\alpha\frac{\alpha!}{m!}\sum_{w:\ \mathrm{content}(w)=\alpha}|w\rangle\in\mathrm{Sym}^m,\qquad
\langle F|F\rangle=\sum_\alpha|f_\alpha|^2\frac{\alpha!}{m!}=\|f\|^2_{\mathrm{BW}},
$$

so the Bombieri–Weyl norm is the natural tensor norm. On symmetric states $a_{j_1}\cdots a_{j_m}=\sqrt{N!/(N-m)!}\,\langle j_1\cdots j_m|_{1..m}\otimes\mathbb 1$, hence

$$
H_N\;\cong\;P_{\mathrm{sym}}\Bigl[\sum_jm_j!\sum_{|S|=m_j}\bigl(|F_j\rangle\langle F_j|\bigr)_S\Bigr]P_{\mathrm{sym}} ,
$$

a permutation-symmetric sum of rank-one $m_j$-body positive terms ($\|f_j\|^2_{\mathrm{BW}}$ times a projector), compressed to the symmetric subspace; individual subset terms do not preserve $\mathrm{Sym}^N$, only the symmetrised sum does. This is a *bosonic analogue* of quantum $k$-SAT: forbidden $m_j$-body states $|F_j\rangle$, but restricted to the symmetric sector and with every forbidden state replicated over all subsets. It is not a Bravyi instance on distinguishable particles. In physical terms the generators are number-conserving multi-boson interactions: for the conic, $H=n_0n_1+n_2(n_2-1)-(a_0^\dagger a_1^\dagger a_2^2+\text{h.c.})$, which is the spin-mixing Hamiltonian of a spin-1 spinor condensate in the single-mode approximation (Law–Pu–Bigelow 1998, with $(z_0,z_1,z_2)\leftrightarrow(a_{+1},a_{-1},a_0)$); its ferromagnetic phase has exactly $2N+1$ degenerate ground states, the Hilbert function of the conic, so this quadric ideal has already been realised in the laboratory. "Local" here means: each term moves $m_j$ bosons (it can change up to $2m_j$ occupation numbers) and is $m_j$-body on the qudit register. It is not local on the qubit register a quantum computer would use (occupation numbers in binary); there $H_N$ is a sparse, efficiently row-computable operator, which is the right notion for QSVT (Section 5).

**Consequences.**

- *Points of the variety are the product states in the ground space.* The normalised spin-coherent state $|p\rangle^{\otimes N}$, $\|p\|=1$, is the polynomial $(p\cdot z)^N/\sqrt{N!}$, and the reproducing property $\langle(p\cdot z)^N,q\rangle=N!\,q(\bar p)$ gives

$$
|p\rangle^{\otimes N}\in\ker H_N\iff q(\bar p)=0\ \ \forall q\in I_N\iff\bar p\in V(I_N),
$$

  with $V(I_N)=V(I)$ for $N\ge\max_jm_j$. A point $x\in V(I)$ corresponds to the physical state $|\bar x\rangle^{\otimes N}$. The energy of a coherent state is a weighted sum of squares of the equations,

$$
\langle p^{\otimes N}|H_N|p^{\otimes N}\rangle=\sum_j\frac{N!}{(N-m_j)!}\,|f_j(\bar p)|^2,\qquad\|p\|=1,
$$

  *numerically* exact to $10^{-15}$, conjugation included (a complex quadric and a point with $f(p)=0\ne f(\bar p)$: the state of $p$ has energy $O(N^2)$, the state of $\bar p$ has energy $10^{-16}$).

- *Reduced structure.* $\operatorname{span}\{|p\rangle^{\otimes N}:\bar p\in V\}=(I(V)_N)^\perp$ with $I(V)$ the vanishing ideal. So the ground space is spanned by coherent states of points of $V$ exactly when $I_N=I(V)_N$ (degreewise; global radicality and saturation need this for all large $N$). Otherwise the ground space contains $\dim I(V)_N-\dim I_N$ additional, necessarily entangled, states carrying non-reduced or irrelevant-torsion structure.

- *Multigraded (Segre) version.* With variables $z_{i,s}$, $i=1..n$ (sites), $s=0..q-1$ (levels), the multidegree $(1,\dots,1)$ sector of $\mathbb{C}[z_{i,s}]$ is $(\mathbb{C}^q)^{\otimes n}$ with $z_{1,s_1}\cdots z_{n,s_n}\leftrightarrow|s_1\cdots s_n\rangle$, orthonormal (all Fock norms are $1$). A multilinear form $f$ on a set $S$ of sites gives

$$
a^\dagger(f)a(f)\big|_{(1,\dots,1)}=|f\rangle\langle f|_S\otimes\mathbb 1
$$

  *exactly*, no factorials (checked to machine precision for 3 to 12 sites; e.g. three generic bilinear forms on the three pairs of a 3-qubit system leave a 2-dimensional inverse system, three singlet projectors leave the 4-dimensional spin-$3/2$ multiplet). Hence every quantum $k$-SAT instance is the $(1,\dots,1)$ block of an ideal generated by $k$-multilinear forms: a rank-$r$ local projector is split into $r$ rank-one ones, which preserves the Hamiltonian and its gap. Product-state solutions are the points of the multiprojective variety $V(I)\subset(\mathbb{P}^{q-1})^n$; entangled solutions are the rest of the inverse system in that multidegree.

**Proposition 3.1 (translation of the QSAT theorems).** For ideals generated by $k$-multilinear forms across $n$ blocks of $q$ variables, the promise problem "$\mathrm{HF}_{R/I}(1,\dots,1)>0$, or the smallest eigenvalue of $H_{(1,\dots,1)}$ is $\ge1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$" is in P for $k=2$, $q=2$ (Bravyi 2006; for bilinear forms across blocks of unequal sizes such as $(2,5)$ it is already $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-complete, Rudolph–Gharibian–Nagaj 2024) and $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-complete for $k=3$, $q=2$ (Gosset–Nagaj 2013); with monomial generators it is classical $k$-SAT, NP-complete for $k\ge3$. Without the NO-gap promise, exact vanishing of the Hilbert function at $(1,\dots,1)$ is not thereby placed in $\mathrm{QMA}_1$. The geometric question $V(I)\ne\emptyset$ (product-state satisfiability) is, by the Nullstellensatz, non-vanishing of the Hilbert function in *all* large multidegrees, not just at $(1,\dots,1)$. This is the cleanest statement of what "encoding a variety in a ground state" buys: the Hilbert function at the multilinear degree is the linear-algebra relaxation of the geometric question, and it is already quantum-hard.

## 4. The notes' second half: a parent Hamiltonian for the ideal itself

The notes try to build $H'$ with $\ker H'=I_N$ (rather than $I_N^\perp$) by summing shifted projectors, $H\mapsto\sum_\alpha J^\alpha HJ^{\alpha\dagger}$, and correctly notice an AND/OR problem. The obstruction is exact: the kernel of a sum of positive operators is the *intersection* of the kernels (AND), whereas $I_N=\operatorname{span}_\beta\{z^\beta f_j\}$ is a *span* (OR); $\bigcap_\alpha J^\alpha(\ker H_0)$ is far too small.

Two notions of locality must be kept apart, and the answer is negative in both, except when the whole ideal lives in boundedly many modes.

**Fact 4.1 (no $k$-body parent Hamiltonian for $I_N$).** Let $0\ne I_N\ne R_N$ and $N\ge k+\min_jm_j$. If $T\ge0$ is a $k$-body operator on the symmetric sector with $T(I_N)=0$, then $T=0$ on $\mathrm{Sym}^N$. Proof sketch: $T$ vanishes on the joint support of the $k$-body marginals of $I_N$; a vector $u\in\mathrm{Sym}^k$ orthogonal to that support satisfies, by the contraction formula of Section 3, $\bar u(\partial)(fh)=0$ for a generator $f$ and all $h\in R_{N-m}$. Take $h=\ell^{N-m}$ with $\ell$ a linear form, $u(\ell)\ne0$, $\ell\nmid f$ (possible since $u\ne0$ and $f$ has finitely many linear factors); in coordinates with $\ell=z_0$ the coefficient of the lowest power $z_0^{N-m-k}$ in $\bar u(\partial)(fz_0^{N-m})$ is $(N-m)_k\,\overline{u(\ell)}\,f|_{z_0=0}\ne0$. So the marginals have full support and $T=0$. This applies to monomial ideals too: for $I=(z_0)$ the projector $P_{I_N^\perp}=(\mathbb 1-|0\rangle\langle0|)^{\otimes N}$ is $N$-body, and $\theta(\hat n_0)$ is not fixed-body local.

**Fact 4.2 (few-mode parents exist only for ideals confined to few modes).** In the mode (second-quantised) sense, if $I$ is generated by forms in the modes of a bounded set $A$ (after a unitary change of variables), then $I_N=\bigoplus_r(I\cap R(A))_r\otimes R(A^c)_{N-r}$ and $\mathbb 1-P_{I\cap R(A)}$ is an $|A|$-mode operator (an unbounded function of the mode operators, legitimate as an on-site term in a Bose–Hubbard sense, but not a local Hamiltonian in the complexity-theoretic sense) with kernel $I_N$. Conversely, a common kernel of positive operators each supported on a bounded mode set is an intersection of few-mode conditions, while $I_N=\sum_j(f_j)_N$ is a union-type (OR) condition: for a monomial ideal whose generator supports cannot be covered by any of the allowed mode sets, e.g. $(z_0z_1,z_2z_3,\dots,z_{2r}z_{2r+1})$ with $r$ large, any few-mode $T\ge0$ annihilating $I_N$ also annihilates every $|k\rangle$ with at least two bosons outside its mode set (extend $k$ outside the set to occupy a generator), so the common kernel is strictly larger than $I_N$. Note the property is not invariant under change of generators, e.g. $(z_0+z_1,z_0-z_1)=(z_0,z_1)$ is confined to two modes.

What *is* available:

- $P_{I_N}=\Theta(H_N)$ by QSVT, at cost $O(\alpha/\Delta_N\cdot\log\epsilon^{-1})$ uses of an $\alpha$-normalised block encoding of $H_N$ (Section 5). Non-local, but efficient whenever $\Delta_N/\alpha$ is not too small.
- The "unitary version of $L_f^\dagger$" the notes ask for is the isometry in the polar decomposition $M_f=U_f|M_f|$ of multiplication by $f$, $M_f:R_{N-m}\to R_N$; $U_f$ maps $R_{N-m}$ isometrically onto $(f)_N$ and is a QSVT singular-value transformation of $M_f$, at cost $\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_{\min}$ of $M_f$, which by Fact 7.1 is $O(N^{m/2})$.
- The shift operators $J_j$ of the notes are the isometric parts of $a_j^\dagger=J_j\sqrt{\hat n_j+1}$, unilateral shifts on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}_+)$. A *graded* subspace invariant under all $a_j^\dagger$ is exactly a homogeneous ideal, since a graded subspace closed under multiplication by each $z_j$ is an ideal; invariance under the $J_j$ is a different condition (the occupation-dependent factor $\sqrt{\hat n_j+1}$ does not preserve a general ideal). Without the grading the invariant-subspace lattice is larger (Beurling's $\theta H^2$ in one variable), and this is Arveson's Hilbert-module setting. Passing to $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})$ (Laurent polynomials) makes the shifts unitary but destroys positivity of the grading and the Fock structure.

## 5. What a quantum computer can do with $H_N$: quantum linear algebra on Macaulay matrices

Let $\Phi_N:\bigoplus_jR_{N-m_j}\to R_N$, $(h_j)\mapsto\sum_jf_jh_j$ be the degree-$N$ Macaulay map in Fock norms. Then $H_N=\Phi_N\Phi_N^\dagger$, $\ker\Phi_N=\mathrm{Syz}(I)_N$, and

$$
\Delta_N:=\lambda^{\ne0}_{\min}(H_N)=\sigma^{\ne0}_{\min}(\Phi_N)^2,\qquad
\|H_N\|\le\sum_j\frac{N!}{(N-m_j)!}\|f_j\|^2_{\mathrm{BW}}\le d\,N^m\max_j\|f_j\|^2_{\mathrm{BW}} .
$$

**Input model and resources.** Generators with $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ monomials and $\mathrm{poly}(n)$-bit coefficients; $N$ given in unary (the tasks below are polynomial in $N$, never in $\log N$). The register holds $k=(k_0,\dots,k_n)$, $\sum k_j=N$, in $O(n\log N)$ qubits. $H_N$ has row sparsity $\le\sum_j(\#\text{monomials of }f_j)^2$ and entries $\sum_j\overline{f_{j,\alpha}}f_{j,\beta}\sqrt{k!\,(k-\alpha+\beta)!}/(k-\alpha)!$, computable reversibly in $\mathrm{poly}(n,\log N)$ time, so a sparse-access block encoding with normalisation $\alpha=O(s\max|H_{xy}|)=\mathrm{poly}(n)\,N^{m}\max\|f_j\|^2$ is available. QSVT then gives, at cost

$$
T=\mathrm{poly}(n^m,d)\times\frac{\alpha}{\Delta_N}\times\log\frac1\epsilon
$$

block-encoding uses, any of: $P_0$ (projector onto $(I_N)^\perp\cong(R/I)_N$), $\Theta(H_N)$ (projector onto $I_N$), the polar isometries $U_f$, and eigenvalue-resolved access to $H_N$. A degree $N$ exponential in $n$ is cheap in *qubits* only: $\alpha/\Delta_N$ and all precision targets scale polynomially in $N$, so the running time is exponential there. All promises below are on the *normalised* gap $\Delta_N/\alpha$; the absolute $\Delta_N$ changes under rescaling generators.

**Task list, with the three cost factors** (spectral $\alpha/\Delta_N$; overlap, i.e. the weight of the prepared state on the target subspace; readout, cheap only for global linear-algebraic quantities).

1. *Distance to the ideal.* Given a sparse $f\in R_N$, estimate $\langle f|P_0|f\rangle/\|f\|^2=\mathrm{dist}_{\mathrm{BW}}(f,I_N)^2/\|f\|^2$ to additive $\epsilon$: cost $\tilde O(T/\epsilon)$ with amplitude estimation. This decides "$f\in I_N$ or $f$ is $\epsilon$-far" but is fundamentally an *estimation* problem, and its hardness (Conjecture 8.1) is stated as such. Exact membership is EXPSPACE-complete (Mayr–Meyer) only for unbounded degree; at fixed $N$ it is linear algebra of dimension $\binom{N+n}{n}$, and the advantage is exponential in $n$ only in the promise regime.
2. *Normalised Hilbert function.* $\mathrm{HF}(N)/\binom{N+n}{n}=\mathrm{Tr}P_0/\dim R_N$ to additive $\epsilon$, by DQC1-style trace estimation (a maximally mixed state on the composition register plus controlled QSVT; membership in DQC1 proper is not claimed), cost $\tilde O(T/\epsilon^2)$. Exact or multiplicative estimates are out of reach (Section 6). The normalised value is informative for low codimension: for $\operatorname{codim}V=c$, $\deg V=D$ and $N$ in the stable range, $\mathrm{HF}(N)/\binom{N+n}{n}\approx D\frac{n!}{(n-c)!}N^{-c}$, so $c$ and $D$ are readable when $c=O(1)$ and the error is below $\frac12\frac{n!}{(n-c)!}N^{-c}$ after controlling lower-order terms.
3. *Random elements.* Applying $P_0$ to the maximally mixed state of $R_N$ yields the mixed state $P_0/\mathrm{HF}(N)$ (not a Haar-random pure quotient element, which is not efficiently preparable); applying $\Theta(H_N)$ yields $P_{I_N}/\dim I_N$. Sampling monomials from these is a "random Macaulay row" sampler whose classical analogue is trivial, so no advantage.
4. *Quantum Stickelberger (direction, not a claim).* For $I$ saturated with $R/I$ a zero-dimensional scheme of length $D$ and $z_0$ a non-zero-divisor, $\mathrm{HF}(N)=D$ for $N\ge\mathrm{reg}$ and the compressed operators $Z_j=P_{0,N+1}a_j^\dagger P_{0,N}$ satisfy $Z_j=Z_0X_j$ with $X_j$ the multiplication by $x_j=z_j/z_0$ transported to $(I_N)^\perp$; the eigenvalues of $X_j$ are the coordinates of the points (Stickelberger), with Jordan blocks at non-reduced points. The $X_j$ commute but are not normal, so phase estimation does not apply; a QSVT singular-value scan of $Z_j-\lambda Z_0$ is the natural substitute, with cost controlled by pseudospectral/eigenvector conditioning, not by coordinate separation alone. The setting is attractive because $D$ may be exponential in $n$ while the register is $O(n\log N)$ qubits; the hardness sits in the overlap factor (Conjecture 8.6).
5. *Adiabatic preparation via Gröbner degeneration.* Take a weight $w$ and generators $g_1,\dots,g_r$ forming a $w$-Gröbner basis of $I$ with $\mathrm{in}_w(g_j)$ single monomials (generic $w$). Scale each monomial $z^\alpha$ of $g_j$ by $t^{\max_{\alpha'}w\cdot\alpha'-w\cdot\alpha}$, $t\in[0,1]$. By Eisenbud Thm 15.17 (after $t\mapsto1/t$) this is a flat family with $I_0=\mathrm{in}_w(I)$ *precisely because the $g_j$ are a Gröbner basis*; for an arbitrary generating set the $t=0$ fibre $(\mathrm{in}_wf_j)$ can be smaller than $\mathrm{in}_w(I)$ and the kernel dimension jumps up there. For $t>0$ the family is a diagonal coordinate change, so constancy of $\dim I_N(t)$ is trivial; the content is $t=0$, where $H_N(0)$ is diagonal with ground states the standard monomials and $\Delta_N(0)\ge\min_j|c_j|^2\alpha^{(j)}!$ in terms of the leading coefficients $c_j$ (presentation-dependent: rescaling a generator changes the gap, not the ideal). *Numerically* (twisted cubic, whose $2\times2$ minors are a Gröbner basis for $w=(0,1,4,9)$, $N\le9$): kernel dimension $3N+1$ for all $t\in[10^{-10},1]$, $\Delta_N(t)$ decreasing monotonically from $\approx0.85N$ at $t=1$ to exactly $1$ at $t=0$, so $\min_t\Delta_N(t)=1$ independent of $N$ and $\min_t\Delta_N(t)/\|H_N(t)\|\approx2.5N^{-1.75}$. Adiabatic evolution transports one initial standard monomial at a time into a ground state; the transported basis is related to any canonical basis of $(R/I)_N$ by the non-abelian holonomy of the path, and the degenerate-ground-space adiabatic theorem applies because the kernel dimension is constant. This "adiabatic Gröbner deformation" looks genuinely new.
6. *Homotopy paths.* For $f_t=(1-t)g+tf$ the Hilbert function of $R/I_t$ is upper semicontinuous in $t$: it can be larger at isolated special $t$ (an excited eigenvalue of $H_N(t)$ touches zero there) and drops back immediately after. Complex $t$ is allowed ($H_N(t)$ stays Hermitian positive). But constancy of the Hilbert function does not see root collisions (the scheme length is constant while roots merge), so this is *not* the discriminant avoidance of Beltrán–Pardo/Lairez; what an adiabatic root tracker would need is control of the spectrum of the compressed multiplication operators along the path, which is a different and harder question.

**5.7 What the distance-to-ideal quantity is, geometrically.** The quantity in task 1, $\delta(f)^2:=\langle f|P_0|f\rangle/\|f\|^2$, looks artificial only if one insists on exact membership. It has several standard readings.

- *Quotient norm.* $\delta(f)=\|[f]\|$ for the Hilbert norm on $(R/I)_N$ induced from Bombieri–Weyl, i.e. the norm of the Drury–Arveson quotient module $H^2_{n+1}/[I]$.
- *Restriction norm of a section to $V$.* The map $f\mapsto(p\mapsto\langle p^{\otimes N}|f\rangle)$ is an isometry onto the reproducing-kernel space of the kernel $\langle p,q\rangle^N$, and for radical $I$ in the stable range $P_0$ is the projector onto the closed span of the kernel functions at points of $V$ (Aronszajn). Hence

$$
\delta(f)^2=\frac{\|f|_V\|^2_{\mathrm{RKHS}(V)}}{\|f\|^2}\;\asymp\;N^{\dim V-n}\,\frac{\int_V|f|^2_{h^N}\,d\mathrm{vol}_V}{\int_{\mathbb P^n}|f|^2_{h^N}\,d\mathrm{vol}},
$$

  with comparison constants from Bergman-kernel asymptotics (Tian–Zelditch–Catlin) and Ohsawa–Takegoshi extension. The promise "$f\in I$ or $\delta(f)\ge\epsilon$" says the hypersurface $\{f=0\}$ either contains $V$ or misses it by a definite $L^2$ amount, a Łojasiewicz-type or quantitative-Nullstellensatz separation. $P_0$ itself is the degree-$N$ Bergman projector of $V$ written in ambient monomial coordinates, so Section 5 is quantum access to the Bergman kernel of a projective variety.
- *Polynomial-kernel machine learning.* The Bombieri–Weyl space is the reproducing-kernel space of the homogeneous polynomial kernel $(x\cdot y)^N$. For a finite point set $X$, $\delta(f)^2\|f\|^2=f_X^\dagger G^{-1}f_X$ with $G$ the coherent-state Gram matrix and $f_X$ the evaluation vector; its diagonal approximation $\sum_{x\in X}|f(x)|^2$ is the objective of approximate vanishing ideals and Vanishing Component Analysis (Heldt–Kreuzer–Pokutta–Poulisse 2009; Livni et al. 2013), where "distance to the vanishing ideal of the data" is the working notion.
- *Ground-space overlap.* In Hamiltonian-complexity terms $\delta(f)^2$ is the weight of a guiding state on the ground space of a gapped frustration-free Hamiltonian, the guided-local-Hamiltonian family of problems (Gharibian–Le Gall 2022 and successors), which is the source of the BQP intuition in Conjecture 8.1.

Once $P_0$ is the Bergman projector, its bilinear and trace versions are geometric quantities that classical methods reach only through root-finding:

- coherent inputs give the Berezin symbol $\langle p^{\otimes N}|P_0|p^{\otimes N}\rangle$, a smooth indicator of $V$ at Fubini–Study resolution $N^{-1/2}$ whose Tian–Zelditch expansion $N^{\dim V}\pi^{-\dim V}(1+S(p)/2N+\dots)$ carries the scalar curvature $S$ of $V$ in the induced Fubini–Study metric (classically easy by differentiating the equations, so no advantage);
- two ideals give $\mathrm{Tr}(P_IP_J)$, which localises on $V\cap W$ at scale $N^{-1/2}$: its growth exponent in $N$ is $\dim(V\cap W)$, and for disjoint varieties it decays like $\cos^{2N}$ of the Fubini–Study distance between them, so the normalised overlap estimates intersection dimension or the distance between two varieties without forming $I+J$;
- Toeplitz traces $\mathrm{Tr}(P_0T_g)/\mathrm{HF}(N)$ with $g(z,\bar z)$ a polynomial, so that $T_g$ is a normal-ordered bosonic observable, converge by Berezin–Toeplitz theory (Bordemann–Meinrenken–Schlichenmaier) to $\int_Vg\,d\mathrm{vol}_V/\mathrm{vol}(V)$: quantum integration over an algebraic variety.

The fair classical competitor for all three is Monte Carlo on $V$: cut with a random linear space of complementary dimension, whose intersection points are Fubini–Study-distributed on $V$ by the kinematic formula, find them by homotopy continuation, and average. Its cost is governed by the condition number of the slice systems (average polynomial for Kostlan-random data, Beltrán–Pardo and Lairez), the quantum cost by $\alpha/\Delta_N$. Whether these two condition numbers are related is the research question this analysis points to (Conjecture 8.11).

## 6. Where speedups cannot come from (hardness anchors)

- **Exact Hilbert function is #P-hard.** The boolean ideal $J=(z_i^2-z_iz_0)_{i=1}^n$ is a radical, saturated complete intersection of $n$ quadrics with $\mathrm{HF}(N)=\sum_{i\le\min(N,n)}\binom ni$, equal to $2^n$ from $N=n$ on (*numerically* confirmed for $n\le5$), and $(R/J)_N$ is the full function algebra on $\{0,1\}^n$ for $N\ge n$. Adjoining homogenised cubic clause forms of a 3-CNF $\phi$ (a clause form vanishing at $[1:x]$ iff the clause is satisfied) gives an ideal $K$ whose dehomogenisation is radical with $V=\{[1:x]:x\models\phi\}$; the homogeneous $K$ is generally neither radical nor saturated (irrelevant torsion), but since cubic multiples generate all functions supported on unsatisfying assignments once $N\ge n+3$, $\mathrm{HF}_{R/K}(N)=\#\mathrm{SAT}(\phi)$ for $N\ge n+3$ (*numerically*: 45 random and constructed instances with $n=3,\dots,7$, exact Gröbner/GF($p$) ranks; stabilisation happens by $N=n+2$, and for $N$ below that the Hilbert function overshoots, e.g. $29,57,71,57,29,9,2,1$ for a unique-solution instance with $n=7$). So an exact quantum evaluation of $\mathrm{HF}$ would give $\#\mathrm{P}\subseteq\mathrm{FBQP}$, and a multiplicative approximation $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\mathrm{BQP}$. Already for squarefree monomial ideals the Hilbert series is #P-hard to evaluate (standard monomials of a graph ideal are independent sets; Dickenstein–Tobis 2012), and there $H_N$ is diagonal. The DQC1-style additive version is the only realistic target.
- **Kernel non-triviality.** $\ker H_N\ne0\iff\phi$ satisfiable is NP-hard; $\ker H_N=0$ is coNP-hard; via Proposition 3.1 the promise version is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-hard. No spectral-gap promise rescues *decision* of frustration-freeness. Hilbert's Nullstellensatz problem (non-emptiness of $V$ over $\mathbb{C}$) is NP-hard and in AM under GRH (Koiran 1996), emptiness in coAM; it becomes "$\ker H_N=0$" at $N\sim d^n$ (Kollár), representable but exponentially expensive in time.
- **Overlap factors.** For a task solved by black-box projection of a prepared state onto a subspace of dimension $\mathrm{HF}(N)$, the cost is at least the inverse root of the state's weight on that subspace; from the maximally mixed state this is $\sqrt{\dim R_N/\mathrm{HF}(N)}$. This is not a universal lower bound: structured ideals may admit efficiently preparable states with large weight on the ground space (Conjecture 8.6 uses exactly this).
- **Prior art on HHL for Macaulay matrices.** Chen–Gao (2018/2022) run an HHL solver on a Macaulay linear system over $\mathbb{C}$ encoding a Boolean system, with cost polynomial in the condition number; Ding–Gheorghiu–Gilyén–Hallgren–Li (Quantum 2023) prove that this condition number is exponentially large unless the solution has Hamming weight $O(\log n)$, so Grover wins. Their system is a different Macaulay matrix (a solve for a particular vector) and there is no established reduction between their condition number and the quantities here; but the lesson is the same: in the hard instances the cost hides in a normalisation, not in a spectral gap, and any claim here must survive that test. The related "bosonic quantum computational complexity" framework (Chabaud–Joseph–Mehraban–Motamedi 2024) concerns unbounded-particle bosonic models and is different from the fixed-$N$ symmetric sector here.
- **Fixed mode number is classically easy.** Permutation-invariant Hamiltonians on $N$ qudits of *fixed* local dimension have ground states and expectation values computable classically in time polynomial in $N$ by Schur-basis block diagonalisation (Anschuetz–Bauer–Kiani–Lloyd 2023). Here the local dimension is $n+1$, so this covers exactly the regime "fixed ideal, growing $N$" and confirms independently that any advantage must come from growing $n$.
- **Dequantisation.** $H_N$ is sparse and high-rank, so low-rank dequantisation does not apply to it; $P_0$ has rank $\mathrm{HF}(N)$, polynomial in $N$ for bounded $\dim V$ but possibly exponential in $n$ through the degree, and classical low-rank methods need sample-and-query access and conditioning besides rank.

## 7. The central unknown: the Macaulay gap $\Delta_N$

Everything with a potential exponential speedup is governed by

$$
\Delta_N=\min\Bigl\{\frac{\|\sum_jf_jh_j\|^2}{\sum_j\|h_j\|^2}:\ (h_j)\in\bigoplus_jR_{N-m_j},\ (h_j)\perp\mathrm{Syz}(I)_N\Bigr\},
$$

a *quantitative syzygy* constant: how close a non-syzygy can come to being one. For one generator this is the Bombieri–Beauzamy–Enflo–Montgomery inequality $[fh]_{\mathrm{BW}}\ge\sqrt{m!(N-m)!/N!}\,[f]_{\mathrm{BW}}[h]_{\mathrm{BW}}$, which in Fock normalisation reads $\|fh\|\ge\|f\|\,\|h\|$:

**Fact 7.1 (principal ideals are uniformly gapped).** For $I=(f)$, $\Delta_N\ge\|f\|^2_{\mathrm{Fock}}=m!\,\|f\|^2_{\mathrm{BW}}$ for all $N\ge m$. The bound is attained whenever, after a unitary change of variables, the extra degree can be placed in a linear subspace orthogonal to the variables $f$ depends on (e.g. $f=z_0^m$, $h=z_1^{N-m}$; or $f=z_0+z_1$, $h=(z_0-z_1)^{N-1}$); in one variable $\Delta_N=N!/(N-m)!$. For the conic, $a(f)a^\dagger(f)=a^\dagger(f)a(f)+\hat n_0+\hat n_1+4\hat n_2+3$ gives $\Delta_N\ge N+1$, and *numerically* $\Delta_N=N+1$ exactly for $N\le30$.

**Fact 7.2 (quadrics: the gap grows at a rate set by the distance to the cones).** Any quadric can be brought by a *unitary* change of variables (Takagi factorisation of its complex symmetric matrix) to $f=\sum_jd_jw_j^2$ with $d_j\ge0$ the Takagi values and $w_j$ orthonormal linear forms; this preserves the Bombieri–Weyl norm and turns $b_j=a(w_j)$ into canonical modes. Then $[a(f),a^\dagger(f)]=\sum_jd_j^2(4\hat n_j+2)$, and since $a(f)a^\dagger(f)=a^\dagger(f)a(f)+[a(f),a^\dagger(f)]$ on $R_{N-2}$,

$$
4d_{\min}^2(N-2)+\|f\|^2_{\mathrm{Fock}}\;\le\;\Delta_N\;\le\;d_{\min}^2N(N-1)+\|f\|^2_{\mathrm{Fock}}-2d_{\min}^2 ,
$$

the upper bound from $h=w_{\min}^{N-2}$. For the conic, $d=(1/2,1/2,1)$ and the lower bound is $N+1$, which is the exact value found numerically. So the gap of a quadric grows at least linearly with slope $4d_{\min}^2$, where $d_{\min}$ is the Bombieri–Weyl distance from $f$ to the quadrics of deficient rank, i.e. to the cones; a cone ($d_{\min}=0$) has bounded gap. This explains the "flat" random quadric in $\mathbb P^4$ in the survey (a $5\times5$ Gaussian symmetric matrix typically has a small Takagi value, so the growth is slow) and the pinned pyramid example below.

For several generators no such inequality seems to be in the literature. $\Delta_N$ depends on the generating tuple, not only on $I$: $(f_1,f_1+\epsilon f_2)$ generates the same ideal as $(f_1,f_2)$ for $\epsilon\ne0$ and has $\Delta_N=O(\epsilon^2)$. What is known or easy:

- monomial ideals: $H_N$ is diagonal with integer entries $\sum_j\prod_ik_i!/(k_i-\alpha^{(j)}_i)!$, so $\Delta_N\ge1$; e.g. $\|H_N\|=N(N-1)(N-2)$ exactly for $(z_0^2,z_0z_1,z_1^3)$;
- binomial (toric) ideals: $H_N$ is exactly block diagonal over the fibres of the $A$-grading; each block is a weighted graph Laplacian on the fibre whose one-dimensional kernel is spanned by the fibre sum $q_u=\sum_{Ak=u}z^k/k!$ (the exponential generating function of the fibre, *numerically* exact), so the degeneracy is the number of fibres and $\Delta_N=\min_u\lambda_2(H_u)$. The fibre graphs get denser faster than they get longer: for the twisted cubic $\Delta_N/N\to0.854$ and for the rational normal quartic $\Delta_N\approx0.61N^{1.05}$, not the naive $N^{-2}$. But growth is not general: for the toric ideal $(x_{00}x_{11}-x_{01}x_{10})$ of the pyramid over a square, with apex variable $y$ absent from the relation, $h=y^{N-2}$ attains Fact 7.1 and $\Delta_N=2$ for all $N$ (*numerically* exact for $N\le8$; the same binomial without $y$ has $\Delta_N=N$ exactly, and adjoining an unused variable to the twisted cubic flattens its gap from $0.85N$ to exactly $2$ for $N\le12$). Any lower bound growing with $N$ must therefore exclude ideals extended from a subring;
- Bombieri–Weyl norms are $U(n+1)$-invariant, so $\Delta_N$ is a unitary invariant of the generator tuple, the quantity that would appear in a Shub–Smale/Bürgisser–Cucker condition theory for the Macaulay map.

**Numerical survey** (exact diagonalisation, dimensions up to a few thousand; unit-coefficient generators as written).

| ideal | $\Delta_N$ | $\|H_N\|$ | $\|H_N\|/\Delta_N$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| conic in $\mathbb P^2$, $N\le30$ | $N+1$ exactly | $\approx N^2$ | $\approx N$ |
| twisted cubic, $N\le22$ | $0.80N^{1.03}$ | $\approx N^2$ | $1.08N^{1.01}$ |
| $(z_0^2,z_0z_1,z_1^3)$, $N\le40$ | $1$ exactly | $N(N-1)(N-2)$ | $\approx0.6N^{3.1}$ |
| rational normal quartic, $N\le13$ | $0.61N^{1.05}$ | $\approx N^2$ | $1.5N^{0.97}$ |
| two generic quadrics in $\mathbb P^3$, $N\le22$ | $0.11N^{0.66}$ | $0.44N^2$ | $3.3N^{1.39}$ |
| boolean ideal, $\mathbb P^3$–$\mathbb P^5$, $N\le14$ | $\to2$, then $N^{0.05\text{–}0.4}$ | $1.3N^2$ | $N^{1.7\text{–}2.1}$ |
| one random quadric in $\mathbb P^2/\mathbb P^3/\mathbb P^4$ | $N^{1.04}/N^{0.56}/\approx1.75$ | $\approx N^2$ | $N^{1.0}/N^{1.5}/N^{1.9}$ |

At fixed $N$ and growing $n$: the boolean ideal has $\Delta_{n+1}=2$ for $n=2,\dots,6$ (numerically, not a theorem), and random quadric ideals at $N=3,4$ have $\Delta\approx0.7$–$1.2$, flat in $n$. For every "algebraic-geometry" family tested, $\Delta_N$ is bounded below by a constant and $\|H_N\|/\Delta_N$ grows like $N^1$ to $N^3$.

The gap does close in the multigraded QSAT sector: a chain of nearest-neighbour singlet projectors on $m$ qubits has $\Delta=9.13m^{-1.97}$ ($m^2\Delta\to\pi^2$, the ferromagnetic Heisenberg gap), and random frustration-free 2-local chains have median $\Delta\approx m^{-3.5}$ over $m\le11$ (a power law fits better than an exponential, but $m\le11$ cannot exclude a slow exponential). Since the multigraded sector realises every frustration-free projector Hamiltonian, $\Delta$ inherits every known pathology, including exponentially small gaps (Proposition 8.3(d)). The numerics suggest a dichotomy: fixed ideal and growing $N$ is benign; growing $n$ with spin-chain-like generators is where hardness lives.

## 8. What is a theorem, what is a conjecture

**Theorems (some new as statements, none deep).** Facts 2.1–2.3, 4.1, 4.2, 7.1, 7.2; Propositions 3.1 and 8.2; the identifications of 5.7 (quotient norm, RKHS restriction norm, polynomial-kernel norm); the coherent-state characterisation $\operatorname{span}\{|p\rangle^{\otimes N}:\bar p\in V\}=(I(V)_N)^\perp$ and hence "$\ker H_N$ is spanned by product states iff $I_N=I(V)_N$"; the toric fibre-sum description of the ground space; flatness and constant kernel dimension along a Gröbner-basis degeneration; Arveson's curvature = leading multiplicity of the Hilbert polynomial (Arveson 2000, Fang 2003).

**Conjecture 8.1 (distance-to-ideal estimation is BQP-hard and in BQP).** Input: multi-homogeneous generators of degree $\le m=O(1)$ in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ variables with $\mathrm{poly}(n)$-bit coefficients of unit Bombieri–Weyl norm, a multidegree sector (or total degree $N\le\mathrm{poly}(n)$), a $\mathrm{poly}(n)$-sparse unit vector $f$ in that sector, thresholds $a<b$ with $b-a\ge1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$, and the promise $\Delta/\alpha\ge1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$ for the Hamiltonian *restricted to that sector*. Decide whether $\langle f|P_0|f\rangle\le a$ or $\ge b$. Containment is Section 5.1 (the block encoding can be restricted to the invariant sector). Hardness sketch: embed the Feynman–Kitaev Hamiltonian $H_{\mathrm{in}}+H_{\mathrm{prop}}$ of a BQP circuit $U=U_T\cdots U_1$ into the $(1,\dots,1)$ sector via Section 3; it is frustration-free with unique ground state (the history state) and gap $\Omega(T^{-2})$ in that sector; for the normalised two-monomial states $f_\pm=(|0,\mathrm{clock}=0\rangle\pm|y,\mathrm{clock}=T\rangle)/\sqrt2$, $\langle f_\pm|P_0|f_\pm\rangle=|1\pm\overline{\langle y|U|0\rangle}|^2/(2(T+1))$, whose difference is $2\,\mathrm{Re}\langle y|U|0\rangle/(T+1)$, and deciding the sign of $\mathrm{Re}\langle y|U|0\rangle$ against a $1/\mathrm{poly}$ threshold is BQP-hard (the standard matrix-element problem). Degree $m=5$ should suffice; $m=2$ is plausible via 2-local frustration-free universality gadgets. This is hardness for *estimating the distance*; the exact "member or $\epsilon$-far" problem is not what the reduction produces.

**Proposition 8.2 (bosonic QSAT is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-hard).** The symmetric-sector analogue of QSAT (ordinary grading, $N=n$ bosons in $2n$ modes, generators of degree $\le3$, promise version) is $\mathrm{QMA}_1$-hard. Reduction: take a Gosset–Nagaj instance on $n$ qubits as multilinear cubic generators in the site-block variables $z_{i,s}$, and add the hard-core generators $z_{i,s}z_{i,s'}$ for all $i$ and all $s\le s'$ (squares included). Every generator preserves the per-site occupation pattern, so $H_n$ is block diagonal in that pattern; on the pattern $(1,\dots,1)$ the hard-core terms vanish and the block is exactly the QSAT Hamiltonian (Section 3); on every other pattern some site holds $\ge2$ bosons and the diagonal hard-core part alone is $\ge1$. Hence $\ker H_n$ is the QSAT ground space and the nonzero spectrum is $\ge\min(\Delta_{\mathrm{QSAT}},1)$. (Without the hard-core terms the construction fails: any state with all bosons in one block is a spurious zero mode.)

**Conjecture 8.3 (gap classes).** Fock normalisation, unit Bombieri–Weyl generators, $N\ge\max m_j$:

(a) *Uniform Bombieri inequality for ideals.* For every fixed generating tuple, $\inf_N\Delta_N>0$: there is $c(f_1,\dots,f_d)>0$ with $\|\sum_jf_jh_j\|^2\ge c\,\mathrm{dist}((h_j),\mathrm{Syz}_N)^2$ for all $N$. This is Fact 7.1 for $d=1$, holds for monomial ideals with monic generators with $c=\min_j\alpha^{(j)}!$ (a lower bound, not necessarily $\inf_N\Delta_N$), and is consistent with all numerics. It is a closed-range statement for the Macaulay map on the graded Fock module and is genuinely open. Consequence: $\alpha/\Delta_N=\mathrm{poly}(N)$ for every fixed ideal, so the Section 5 tasks are polynomial in $N$ at fixed $I$ (with $N$ in unary), and quantum advantage is entirely a question of the dependence on $n$ and the generator structure.

(b) *Cones versus growth.* If $I$ is extended from a subring, i.e. after a unitary change of variables the generators omit a variable $w$ (so $V$ is a cone with vertex the point dual to $w$), then $h_j=w^{N-m_j}$ shows $\Delta_N\le\min_j\|f_j\|^2$ up to the syzygy correction: the gap is pinned to a constant (the pyramid example, and the twisted cubic with a dummy variable, both numerically flat at $2$). Conjecture: conversely, for a hypersurface $\Delta_N\to\infty$ linearly iff $V(f)$ is not a cone, i.e. iff the partials $\partial_0f,\dots,\partial_nf$ are linearly independent, with slope controlled by the Bombieri–Weyl distance from $f$ to the locus of cones (for quadrics this is Fact 7.2 with slope $4d_{\min}^2$); and for toric ideals $\Delta_N=\min_u\lambda_2(H_u)$, a weighted Poincaré constant of the fibre graphs, grows linearly iff the toric variety is not a cone.

(c) *Generic forms.* For $d\le n+1$ independent Kostlan-random forms of degree $m$ normalised to unit Bombieri–Weyl norm, $\Pr[\inf_{N\ge m}\Delta_N\ge c(m)]\to1$ as $n\to\infty$, with $c(m)>0$ independent of $n$. Evidence is thin (flat in $n$ at $N=3,4$; $\Delta_N\ge0.1N^{0.66}$ for two generic quadrics in $\mathbb P^3$). The natural proof route is a least-singular-value estimate for the structured random Macaulay map in the Bombieri–Weyl basis, where $U(n+1)$-invariance makes the distribution tractable.

(d) *Hard instances exist (essentially a theorem).* For every fixed $m\ge2$ there are ideals generated by $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ forms of degree $m$ with $\Delta/\alpha\le2^{-\Omega(n)}$ in a multigraded sector specified by polynomially many sites (of dimension $q^n$): the area-weighted Motzkin chains of Levine–Movassagh (2017) are 2-local, frustration-free, with gap $\le8ns\,t^{-n^2/3}$, and the multigraded embedding transfers the spectrum; for $m>2$ pad each term by a complete rank-one decomposition of the identity on $m-2$ auxiliary sites. So there is no general polynomial-time quantum algorithm for the Section 5 tasks without a gap promise. Random frustration-free 2-local chains appear to be only polynomially gapped ($\approx m^{-3.5}$ numerically), so typical multigraded instances are not the hard ones.

(e) *Presentation dependence (question, not conjecture).* $c$ in (a) is a property of the tuple. Is there a lower bound on $\inf_N\Delta_N$ in terms of finitely many degrees, e.g. $\min_{N\le N_0}\Delta_N$ with $N_0=\mathrm{reg}(I)+\max m_j$, or in terms of the Bombieri–Weyl distance of the tuple to the locus of tuples with an extra syzygy in degrees $\le N_0$? Whether $\inf_N\Delta_N$ is attained at finite $N$ or only asymptotically is open in both directions.

**Conjecture 8.4 (adiabatic Gröbner deformation).** Fix a reduced $w$-Gröbner basis $G$ of $I$ with unit Bombieri–Weyl elements and the one-parameter degeneration of 5.5. (a) For toric ideals, determinantal ideals of generic matrices, and complete intersections of $d\le n$ forms, $\min_{t\in[0,1]}\Delta_N(t)$ is attained at the monomial end $t=0$, where $\Delta_N(0)\ge\min_j|c_j|^2\alpha^{(j)}!$ ($c_j$ the leading coefficients; equality is not general, e.g. $f=x^2y+xy^2$ with $w_x>w_y$ has $\Delta_4(0)=4|c|^2$), so $\min_t\Delta_N(t)$ is bounded below independently of $N$ while $\|H_N(t)\|=O(N^m)$; hence each standard monomial can be transported adiabatically to a ground state of $H_N$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(N)$. Evidence: the twisted cubic, $\min_t\Delta_N(t)=\Delta_N(0)=1$ for $N=3,\dots,9$, with $\Delta_N(t)$ monotone in $t$. Neither flatness nor a fixed ideal gives spectral monotonicity in $t$ by itself. (b) Define the adiabatic Gröbner complexity of $I$ at degree $N$ as $\min_w\max_{t\in[0,1]}\alpha(t)/\Delta_N(t)$, the minimum over weight vectors $w$ in the open cones of the Gröbner fan (monomial initial ideal), with $G$ the corresponding reduced Gröbner basis normalised to unit Bombieri–Weyl norm and $\alpha(t)$ the block-encoding normalisation. Conjecture: it is polynomial in $(n,N)$ for the classes in (a) and exponential in $n$ for the instances of 8.3(d).

**Conjecture 8.5 (entangled defect).** The theorem part is in the list above; the conjectural part is descriptive: for zero-dimensional $I$ in the stable range (where irrelevant torsion has vanished), $\dim I(V)_N-\dim I_N=\operatorname{length}(\operatorname{Proj}R/I)-\#V$, and the ground-space vectors orthogonal to all coherent states of points of $V$ are spanned by the local inverse systems at the non-reduced points, i.e. by the jets $D(\partial_p)|p\rangle^{\otimes N}$ of coherent states along the differential functionals in Macaulay's local dual at each point (first derivatives for curvilinear double points, higher jets in general). A quantum radicality test does not follow: the coherent states of the points are non-orthogonal with a Gram matrix that can be exponentially ill-conditioned, and a root-sampling oracle does not give the projector onto their span.

**Conjecture 8.6 (root sampling is Grover-limited).** For the boolean-plus-clause ideal $K$ of Section 6 with $D$ solutions and $N\ge n+3$: (i) $\Delta_N(K)/\alpha\ge1/\mathrm{poly}(n)$. *Numerically*, at $N=n+3$ over 54 instances with $n=3,\dots,8$ (random 3-CNF at clause ratios $2$ and $4.2$, UNSAT instances, unique-solution instances) the smallest nonzero eigenvalue stays in $[0.29,2.3]$ while $2^n$ grows by $32\times$; exponential and power-law fits are both statistically empty ($R^2\le0.16$), and the pure boolean part has $\Delta=2$ throughout. The normalised quantity $\|H_N\|/\Delta_N$ does grow, $2.8\times10^3\to2.0\times10^4$, fitting $n^{2.1}$ and $e^{0.43n}$ about equally well over six points, because $\|H_N\|\approx4.2n$ cubics of norm $N^3$; so the gap is not where the hardness is, but the normalised-gap conjecture is still only supported, not established; (ii) for $N=\Omega(n\log(n/\varepsilon))$ the Gram matrix of the $2^n$ coherent states $|\hat p_x\rangle^{\otimes N}$, $\hat p_x=(1,x)/\sqrt{1+|x|}$, is $\varepsilon$-close to the identity in operator norm (nearest neighbours have overlap $\approx e^{-N/(2n)}$, and the number of neighbours at Hamming distance $r$ is $\binom nr$), so $\Psi\propto\sum_x|\hat p_x\rangle^{\otimes N}$ is normalised by $2^{-n/2}$ up to $O(\varepsilon)$ (the logarithmic factor is necessary: *numerically* the smallest Gram eigenvalue of the full hypercube family is $3.5e^{-0.85n}$ at $N=n+3$ and still $0.7e^{-0.35n}$ at $N=2n$, while the Gram matrix restricted to the satisfying assignments of a random instance is well conditioned, $\lambda_{\min}\ge0.13$; at $N=n+3$ the weight $\langle\Psi|P_0|\Psi\rangle$ exceeds $D/2^n$ by factors $1$–$12$); $\Psi$ is preparable to trace distance $O(\sqrt{\varepsilon})$ by preparing $\sum_x|x\rangle|\hat p_x\rangle^{\otimes N}$ and uncomputing $x$ from the occupation pattern, which determines it except with probability $\le n\,e^{-N/(n+1)}$; and $\Psi$ has weight $D/2^n+O(\varepsilon)$ on $\ker H_N(K)$; (iii) hence projecting $\Psi$ onto the ground space and reading a root off the occupation pattern samples a solution, uniformly up to $O(\varepsilon)$, in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,N)\cdot\alpha/\Delta_N\cdot\sqrt{2^n/D}$. This is an upper bound achieving Grover's scaling for this projection method; no lower bound against other algorithms is claimed. Starting instead from the maximally mixed state gives weight $D/\binom{2n+3}{n}\approx D\,4^{-n}$, which is *worse* than Grover; the earlier draft got this wrong. The Chen–Gao/Ding et al. obstruction is the analogous statement for their Macaulay linear system.

**Conjecture 8.7 (normalised Hilbert function and low codimension).** For ideals with $\operatorname{codim}V=c=O(1)$, regularity $\le\mathrm{poly}(n)$ and $N\ge\mathrm{poly}(n)$ in the stable range, a DQC1-style estimate of $\mathrm{HF}(N)/\dim R_N$ to additive error below $\frac12\frac{n!}{(n-c)!}N^{-c}$ at a few values of $N$ determines $c$ and $\deg V$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,N,\alpha/\Delta_N)$, polynomial for the classes in 8.3(c). Classically, the Hilbert polynomial of $d\le n+1$ *generic* forms is known (Koszul), so any advantage is for *structured* low-codimension ideals with many generators and non-trivial syzygies, whose Hilbert functions are not known in closed form (the regime of Fröberg's conjecture, though the additive precision is far too coarse to test that conjecture).

**Conjecture 8.8 (finite fields: no compact analogue).** Macaulay's duality survives over $\mathbb{F}_q$ via the divided-power/contraction action (ordinary differentiation fails, $\partial x^p=0$), and it is realised quantumly as Fourier duality between the uniform superpositions over $I_N$ and over its contraction-dual, on the additive group $\mathbb{F}_q^{\dim R_N}$ (with the syzygy module as the hidden subgroup of the explicitly known map $(h_j)\mapsto\sum h_jf_j$, which yields no advantage over finite-field Gaussian elimination). That register has $\dim R_N\log q$ qubits. The compact amplitude encoding of the complex construction cannot be transported because $\mathbb{F}_q$ does not embed in $\mathbb{C}$ as a field, so complex linear algebra does not preserve $\mathbb{F}_q$-ranks or products; the realistic finite-field analogue is HHL/QSVT on complex Macaulay systems that *encode* $\mathbb{F}_q$-constraints (Chen–Gao), with the Ding et al. obstruction. This is the one direction in the notes that I think does not work.

**Conjecture 8.9 (operator-theoretic dictionary as a conditioning statement).** The compressed shifts $Z_j$ on the ground spaces commute exactly; the Arveson–Douglas conjecture says $[Z_i,Z_j^\dagger]$ is Schatten-$p$ for $p>\dim V+1$ (affine-cone dimension), i.e. the tuple is *asymptotically normal* as $N\to\infty$. Conjecture (zero-dimensional case only, where $X_j=\mu_{z_0}^{-1}\mu_{z_j}$ is defined on a fixed stabilised ground space): for a reduced zero-dimensional scheme with $z_0$ a non-zero-divisor, the eigenvector condition number of the commuting tuple $(X_j)$ on $(I_N)^\perp$, $N\ge\mathrm{reg}$, is bounded by a polynomial in $N$, the inverse coordinate separation of the points, the coefficient height and the presentation conditioning $\alpha/\Delta_N$; for non-reduced points it degrades with the local multiplicity through the Jordan structure. This is what would make the direction 5.4 well-conditioned in the reduced case. Essential normality alone gives no finite-$N$ bound, and ordinary phase estimation does not apply to non-normal $Z_j$, so this is a conjecture about pseudospectra, not a consequence of the Arveson–Douglas theory.

**Conjecture 8.10 (geometric overlaps).** For radical $I,J$ with smooth $V,W$ in the stable range: (a) $\mathrm{Tr}(P_IP_J)=c\,N^{\dim(V\cap W)}(1+O(1/N))$ when $V\cap W\ne\emptyset$ is transversal, with $c$ a positive combination of the degrees of the components of $V\cap W$ and angle factors; (b) $\mathrm{Tr}(P_IP_J)\le\mathrm{HF}_I(N)\,\mathrm{HF}_J(N)\cos^{2N}d_{FS}(V,W)$ when $V\cap W=\emptyset$, with $-\frac1{2N}\log\mathrm{Tr}(P_IP_J)\to\log\sec d_{FS}(V,W)$; (c) for $g$ a polynomial in $z,\bar z$, $\mathrm{Tr}(P_0T_g)/\mathrm{HF}(N)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}V}\int_Vg\,d\mathrm{vol}_V+O(1/N)$ with the $1/N$ term given by the Berezin–Toeplitz expansion (Laplacian and scalar-curvature corrections). All three are DQC1-style estimable in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,N,\alpha/\Delta_N,1/\epsilon)$ to additive $\epsilon$ in the normalised form; (a) and (c) are informative for low codimension.

**Conjecture 8.11 (Macaulay gap versus condition number).** For a reduced complete intersection $V$ of $d\le n$ forms of degree $m$ with unit Bombieri–Weyl norm and Bürgisser–Cucker condition number $\mu_{\max}(V)=\sup_{p\in V}\mu_{\mathrm{norm}}(f,p)$, the normalised gap satisfies $\Delta_N/\alpha\ge1/\mathrm{poly}(n,N,\mu_{\max})$; conversely, singular $V$ (infinite $\mu_{\max}$) forces $\Delta_N/\alpha\to0$ faster than any power of $N$ only through cone-type degenerations, so that $\liminf_N\Delta_N>0$ still holds (Conjecture 8.3(a)). If true, the quantum cost of the Bergman-projector tasks of 5.7 and the classical cost of Monte Carlo on $V$ by random slicing and homotopy continuation are controlled by the same condition quantity, and the comparison reduces to polynomial factors.

## 9. Corrections and small remarks on the notes

1. $\{z:\sum|z_j|^2=1\}$ is $S^{2n+1}$, not $\mathbb{CP}^n$; the associated qudit has dimension $n+1$.
2. $L^2(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ should be the holomorphic (Hardy/Bergman/Fock) space; $h_N=H^0(\mathcal O(N))=\mathrm{Sym}^N\mathbb{C}^{n+1}$.
3. $a(f)$ must carry conjugated coefficients, $a(f)=\bar f(\partial)$, for $H$ to be the positive operator $\sum a^\dagger(f_j)a(f_j)$; with the sphere normalisation the adjoint of $z_j$ is $\partial_j(D+n)^{-1}$, harmless within a fixed degree. Points of $V$ correspond to coherent states of the *conjugate* vectors.
4. "$1-2\overset?=-1$" should be $1+2=3=\|f\|^2$.
5. "$\ker(H+H')\subset\ker H$" and the perturbation remark are right; the clean test is $f\in I_N\iff P_0|f\rangle=0$, and its quantitative version is the estimation problem of Conjecture 8.1.
6. The AND/OR issue is exactly intersection versus span of kernels. Monomial ideals escape only in the few-mode (number-operator) sense; no fixed-body parent exists even for $(z_0)$. The general substitute is $\Theta(H_N)$ via QSVT.
7. "This should allow finite field": see Conjecture 8.8.
8. The boolean ideal's Hilbert function reaches $2^n$ at $N=n$, not $n+1$.
